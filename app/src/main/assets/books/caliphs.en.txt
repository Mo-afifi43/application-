# The Rightly Guided Caliphs

== After the Prophet ﷺ: the day of Saqifah
The Prophet ﷺ died on a Monday in the eleventh year of the Hijrah, and before he was buried the community faced a question it had never faced: who would lead? He had left no written instruction, but he had left many signs — he had appointed Abu Bakr to lead the prayer in his illness, and he had said that Allah and the believers would accept none but Abu Bakr.
The Helpers of Madinah gathered at the shelter of Banu Sa'idah to consider their chief, Sa'd ibn Ubadah. Abu Bakr, Umar and Abu Ubaydah went to them. Abu Bakr spoke with calm and honesty: he praised the Helpers, reminded them that the Arabs would accept leadership only from Quraysh, and offered them the choice between Umar and Abu Ubaydah. Umar answered by taking Abu Bakr's hand and pledging allegiance to him, and the Helpers followed one after another.
The next day, in the mosque, the general pledge was given. Abu Bakr climbed the pulpit and delivered the first speech of the caliphate:
> أَيُّهَا النَّاسُ، إِنِّي قَدْ وُلِّيتُ عَلَيْكُمْ وَلَسْتُ بِخَيْرِكُمْ، فَإِنْ أَحْسَنْتُ فَأَعِينُونِي، وَإِنْ أَسَأْتُ فَقَوِّمُونِي... أَطِيعُونِي مَا أَطَعْتُ اللهَ وَرَسُولَهُ، فَإِذَا عَصَيْتُ اللهَ وَرَسُولَهُ فَلا طَاعَةَ لِي عَلَيْكُمْ.
~ Abu Bakr's inaugural address — "O people, I have been given authority over you, and I am not the best of you. If I do well, help me; and if I do wrong, set me right... Obey me as long as I obey Allah and His Messenger; and if I disobey Allah and His Messenger, you owe me no obedience."
With those words the caliphate was defined: a trust, not a throne; accountable, not absolute; legitimate only through obedience to Allah.
## Lessons
- The companions disagreed and then united within a day, because none of them was seeking power for himself.
- Leadership in Islam is service under the law, and the ruler is the first to be bound by it.

== Abu Bakr: holding the nation together
Abu Bakr ruled for a little over two years, and they were among the most decisive in Islamic history. His first act was to send out the army of Usamah ibn Zayd, which the Prophet ﷺ had prepared, even though Madinah was threatened; he would not undo an order of the Messenger of Allah.
Then the tribes of Arabia rebelled. Some followed false prophets — Musaylimah in Yamamah, Tulayhah, Sajah — and others refused to pay zakat while claiming to remain Muslim. Umar himself hesitated, but Abu Bakr declared: "By Allah, if they withhold from me a rope they used to give to the Messenger of Allah, I will fight them for it." The gentle old man became the firmest of them all. Within a year the rebellions were crushed, and the Arabian Peninsula was united again under Islam.
The Battle of Yamamah was so costly in the lives of Qur'an memorisers that Umar urged Abu Bakr to gather the Qur'an into one book. Abu Bakr appointed Zayd ibn Thabit to collect every verse from the written pieces and the memories of the companions, verified by two witnesses for each. The result was the master copy kept with Abu Bakr, then Umar, then Hafsah.
In the last months of his life, his armies entered Iraq under Khalid and Syria under several commanders. When he fell ill, he consulted the senior companions and nominated Umar. He died in the thirteenth year and was buried beside the Prophet ﷺ, having ordered that everything he had taken from the treasury be returned.
## Lessons
- Mercy and firmness are not opposites; the right one for each moment is wisdom.
- The Book of Allah was preserved because a caliph listened to a good suggestion.

== Umar: justice and expansion
Umar ruled for ten years and built the state that carried Islam to the world. Under his command the Muslims defeated the two superpowers of the age: the Byzantines at Yarmuk and the Persians at Qadisiyyah and Nahavand. Damascus, Jerusalem, Egypt and the Persian heartland all came under Muslim rule.
Yet the greatness of Umar was not conquest but justice. He said: "If a mule stumbled in Iraq, I would fear that Allah would ask me why I had not paved the road for it." He walked at night to check on the poor, carried food on his own back, and wore a patched cloak while receiving the ambassadors of empires. When he came to receive the keys of Jerusalem, he entered walking while his servant rode the camel, because it was the servant's turn.
He established the institutions of the state: the Hijri calendar, the public treasury and the register of stipends, the judiciary with independent judges, the military garrison towns of Kufa and Basra, and a policy of leaving conquered farmers on their land so that future generations would benefit. In the year of famine he refused to eat butter or meat until the people ate, and his skin turned dark from hunger.
He was assassinated by a Persian slave while leading the dawn prayer in the twenty-third year. Dying, he appointed a council of six to choose his successor, and asked Aisha's permission to be buried beside his two companions. The Prophet ﷺ had said: "Allah placed truth on the tongue and heart of Umar."
## Lessons
- Power is only safe in the hands of one who fears the account of the Hereafter more than the loss of power.
- Institutions outlive men; Umar built for the long future.

== Uthman: the Mushaf and the expansion
The council chosen by Umar deliberated for three days and chose Uthman, the gentle and wealthy companion who had married two daughters of the Prophet ﷺ. He ruled for twelve years, the first six of which were years of peace and expansion: the Muslim navy was established, Cyprus and Armenia were opened, North Africa and Khorasan came under Muslim rule.
His greatest legacy was the standard Mushaf. As the community spread across many lands, differences appeared in recitation between regions, and the companion Hudhayfah warned Uthman that people would dispute over the Book of Allah as earlier nations had. Uthman took the master copy from Hafsah, appointed Zayd ibn Thabit and three others from Quraysh to prepare copies in the dialect of Quraysh, and sent one to each major city with a reciter to teach it. Every Qur'an in the world today descends from that project.
In his later years, the vast empire brought new tensions: complaints about governors, tribal rivalries and agitators who stirred the provinces. Rebels from Egypt, Kufa and Basra marched on Madinah and besieged his house for weeks. He refused to let the companions fight for him, saying he would not be the cause of Muslim blood being shed. He was killed in his home while reading the Qur'an, in the thirty-fifth year, an old man of over eighty.
The Prophet ﷺ had told him that Allah would give him a shirt — the caliphate — and that if people asked him to take it off, he should not. He did not.
## Lessons
- Uthman chose to be wronged rather than to wrong; he preferred the safety of the nation to his own.
- The unity of the Qur'an is a gift of his wisdom.

== Ali and Hasan: trials and reconciliation
Ali was given the pledge in Madinah amid chaos. The murderers of Uthman were mixed among the crowds, and every path forward was full of pain. Aisha, Talhah and al-Zubayr demanded swift justice for Uthman; Ali wanted first to establish order. The two sides met near Basra in the Battle of the Camel; peace had almost been agreed when the agitators of the night forced the fighting, and Talhah and al-Zubayr were killed. Ali honoured Aisha and sent her back to Madinah with respect.
Mu'awiyah, governor of Syria and a relative of Uthman, refused allegiance until the killers were punished, and the armies met at Siffin. When the fighting became unbearable, the two sides agreed to arbitration. A group of Ali's own soldiers rejected this and left him — the Khawarij, who declared other Muslims disbelievers. Ali debated them, then fought those who turned to violence, at Nahrawan. One of them killed him with a poisoned sword as he walked to the mosque of Kufa in the fortieth year.
Ali's son Hasan was given the pledge in Kufa. Rather than continue the war, he negotiated with Mu'awiyah and handed over the caliphate in the forty-first year, which was called the Year of Unity. The Prophet ﷺ had said of him decades earlier:
> إِنَّ ابْنِي هَذَا سَيِّدٌ، وَلَعَلَّ اللهَ أَنْ يُصْلِحَ بِهِ بَيْنَ فِئَتَيْنِ عَظِيمَتَيْنِ مِنَ المُسْلِمِينَ.
~ Bukhari — "This son of mine is a sayyid, and perhaps Allah will reconcile through him two great parties of the Muslims."
## How the Sunnah teaches us to remember these years
- The companions who disagreed were sincere; each acted on his own reasoning, and Allah rewards the one who strives even when he errs.
- We love them all, ask Allah to be pleased with them, and hold our tongues about their disputes, as the scholars of Ahl al-Sunnah taught.
- The rightly guided caliphate lasted thirty years, as the Prophet ﷺ had foretold, and left behind an example of leadership by trust, justice and consultation.
