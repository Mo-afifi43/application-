# Prophetic Manners

== Truthfulness
Before he was a prophet, Muhammad ﷺ was al-Sadiq, the truthful. When he stood on the hill of Safa and asked Quraysh whether they would believe him if he warned them of an army behind the mountain, his enemies answered: "We have never known you to lie." Truthfulness was the foundation on which the message stood.
> عَلَيْكُمْ بِالصِّدْقِ، فَإِنَّ الصِّدْقَ يَهْدِي إِلَى البِرِّ، وَإِنَّ البِرَّ يَهْدِي إِلَى الجَنَّةِ، وَمَا يَزَالُ الرَّجُلُ يَصْدُقُ وَيَتَحَرَّى الصِّدْقَ حَتَّى يُكْتَبَ عِنْدَ اللهِ صِدِّيقًا.
~ Bukhari and Muslim — "Hold to truthfulness, for truthfulness leads to righteousness, and righteousness leads to Paradise. A man keeps telling the truth and seeking the truth until he is written with Allah as a siddiq."
The Prophet ﷺ never told a lie, not even in jest; when he joked, he joked with the truth. He forbade the small lies people excuse: telling a child "come and I will give you something" with no intention of giving; embellishing a story to make people laugh; and claiming what one does not have.
Truthfulness is also in dealings: an honest merchant, he said, will be with the prophets and martyrs; and a seller who hides a defect is not one of us.
## Practice
- If you cannot say the truth, stay silent; do not lie.
- Keep your promises even when they cost you.
- Admit mistakes quickly; a truthful admission is more honourable than a clever excuse.

== Greeting and the manners of speech
The salam was the first instruction the Prophet ﷺ gave when he arrived in Madinah: "Spread the greeting of peace, feed people, keep the ties of kinship, and pray at night while people sleep — you will enter Paradise in peace."
> لا تَدْخُلُونَ الجَنَّةَ حَتَّى تُؤْمِنُوا، وَلا تُؤْمِنُوا حَتَّى تَحَابُّوا، أَوَلا أَدُلُّكُمْ عَلَى شَيْءٍ إِذَا فَعَلْتُمُوهُ تَحَابَبْتُمْ؟ أَفْشُوا السَّلامَ بَيْنَكُمْ.
~ Muslim — "You will not enter Paradise until you believe, and you will not believe until you love one another. Shall I tell you of something which, if you do it, you will love one another? Spread the salam among you."
"As-salamu alaykum wa rahmatullahi wa barakatuh" is a prayer of peace, mercy and blessing. The one on a mount greets the one walking, the walker greets the seated, the small group greets the large, and the young greet the old; and everyone replies with the same or better.
The Prophet's speech was measured. He spoke clearly, so that whoever listened could memorise; he did not interrupt; he turned his whole body to the person speaking to him; he did not raise his voice in the market; and he never spoke an obscene word. He said: "The believer is not one who slanders, curses, or speaks obscenely."
## Practice
- Greet first; the best of the two is the one who begins with salam.
- Listen fully before answering; look at the person speaking.
- Choose the gentler of two words. Gentleness beautifies everything it touches.

== Kindness to parents
The Qur'an places kindness to parents immediately after the worship of Allah, and forbids even a sigh of irritation at them:
> وَقَضَىٰ رَبُّكَ أَلَّا تَعْبُدُوا إِلَّا إِيَّاهُ وَبِالْوَالِدَيْنِ إِحْسَانًا ۚ إِمَّا يَبْلُغَنَّ عِندَكَ الْكِبَرَ أَحَدُهُمَا أَوْ كِلَاهُمَا فَلَا تَقُل لَّهُمَا أُفٍّ وَلَا تَنْهَرْهُمَا وَقُل لَّهُمَا قَوْلًا كَرِيمًا
~ Qur'an 17:23 — "Your Lord has decreed that you worship none but Him, and to parents, good treatment. If one or both of them reach old age with you, do not say to them 'uff', and do not repel them, but speak to them a noble word."
A man came to the Prophet ﷺ asking permission to join a military expedition. He asked: "Are your parents alive?" "Yes." "Then strive in serving them." Another man carried his mother on his back around the Ka'bah and asked whether he had repaid her; the answer was that he had not repaid a single contraction of her labour.
The mother is given three shares of companionship for every one the father receives, because of pregnancy, birth and nursing. Yet the father's pleasure is the pleasure of Allah, and his anger is the anger of Allah.
Kindness continues after their death: praying for them, giving charity on their behalf, keeping their promises and honouring their friends.
## Practice
- Call and visit often; presence is the gift they want most.
- Never argue back in anger; lower the wing of humility.
- If they are non-Muslim, kindness and respect are still obligatory, without obedience in sin.

== The rights of neighbours
The Prophet ﷺ said that Jibril kept advising him about neighbours until he thought a neighbour would be made an heir. He swore three times: "By Allah, he does not believe! By Allah, he does not believe! By Allah, he does not believe!" They asked: who, O Messenger of Allah? He said: "The one whose neighbour is not safe from his harm."
> مَا زَالَ جِبْرِيلُ يُوصِينِي بِالجَارِ حَتَّى ظَنَنْتُ أَنَّهُ سَيُوَرِّثُهُ.
~ Bukhari and Muslim — "Jibril kept advising me about the neighbour until I thought he would make him an heir."
A neighbour has rights whether Muslim or not, relative or stranger. The Prophet ﷺ told Abu Dharr: "When you cook a stew, add more water and share it with your neighbours." He warned that a woman who fasts and prays but harms her neighbours with her tongue is in the Fire.
The rights of a neighbour include greeting him, visiting him when he is ill, congratulating him in joy, consoling him in loss, covering his faults, helping him in need, not building in a way that blocks his air or light without permission, and not troubling him with noise.
## Practice
- Learn your neighbours' names and greet them.
- Share food on occasions and check on the elderly among them.
- Be the neighbour whose kindness makes people love Islam.

== Manners of eating and drinking
The Prophet ﷺ taught a young boy who was reaching across the plate: "Say Bismillah, eat with your right hand, and eat from what is nearest to you." Three rules that cover most of the etiquette of the table.
> مَا مَلأَ آدَمِيٌّ وِعَاءً شَرًّا مِنْ بَطْنٍ، بِحَسْبِ ابْنِ آدَمَ أُكُلاتٌ يُقِمْنَ صُلْبَهُ، فَإِنْ كَانَ لا مَحَالَةَ فَثُلُثٌ لِطَعَامِهِ، وَثُلُثٌ لِشَرَابِهِ، وَثُلُثٌ لِنَفَسِهِ.
~ Tirmidhi — "No human fills a vessel worse than his stomach. A few morsels are enough to keep a person's back straight; and if he must, then a third for his food, a third for his drink and a third for his breath."
He sat humbly to eat, saying: "I eat as a servant eats." He ate with three fingers, licked them after eating, did not waste a fallen morsel but cleaned and ate it, and did not blow into food or drink. He drank sitting, in three breaths, and said Alhamdulillah after each. He never criticised food: if he liked it he ate it, and if not, he left it silently.
He loved to eat together: "Gather over your food and mention the name of Allah, and it will be blessed for you."
## Practice
- Bismillah before, Alhamdulillah after, right hand always.
- Stop before you are full; the body and the heart both become lighter.
- Never waste. Food is provision from Allah, and the poor are waiting for it.

== Visiting the sick
Visiting the sick is a right of every Muslim upon every Muslim, alongside returning the greeting, following the funeral, accepting the invitation and answering the one who sneezes. The Prophet ﷺ described its reward as walking in the harvest of Paradise until one returns.
> إِنَّ اللهَ عَزَّ وَجَلَّ يَقُولُ يَوْمَ القِيَامَةِ: يَا ابْنَ آدَمَ مَرِضْتُ فَلَمْ تَعُدْنِي... أَمَا عَلِمْتَ أَنَّ عَبْدِي فُلانًا مَرِضَ فَلَمْ تَعُدْهُ؟ أَمَا عَلِمْتَ أَنَّكَ لَوْ عُدْتَهُ لَوَجَدْتَنِي عِنْدَهُ؟
~ Muslim — "Allah will say on the Day of Resurrection: O son of Adam, I was sick and you did not visit Me... Did you not know that My servant so-and-so was sick and you did not visit him? Did you not know that had you visited him you would have found Me with him?"
The Prophet ﷺ would sit by the sick, place his hand on the forehead, ask how they were, and pray: "La ba's, tahurun in sha Allah" — no harm, it is a purification if Allah wills. He would say seven times: "I ask Allah the Mighty, Lord of the mighty Throne, to cure you," and recite the protecting surahs.
Visits should be short unless the sick person wants company, cheerful, and free of frightening talk. Bringing something the sick person can eat is a Sunnah of generosity.
## Practice
- Visit within the first days of illness; a message is good, a visit is better.
- Give hope, remind of reward and make dua with the sick person.
- Do not forget the family caring for the sick; they need support too.

== Controlling anger
A man asked the Prophet ﷺ for advice, and he said: "Do not get angry." He repeated the request, and received the same answer: "Do not get angry." Anger is a coal that Shaytan blows on, and most family breakups, broken friendships and words that can never be taken back begin with it.
> لَيْسَ الشَّدِيدُ بِالصُّرَعَةِ، إِنَّمَا الشَّدِيدُ الَّذِي يَمْلِكُ نَفْسَهُ عِنْدَ الغَضَبِ.
~ Bukhari and Muslim — "The strong person is not the one who overpowers others in wrestling; the strong one is he who controls himself when angry."
The Prophet ﷺ gave practical medicine for anger: seek refuge in Allah from Shaytan; if standing, sit; if sitting, lie down; make wudu, for anger is from fire and water extinguishes fire; and stay silent. He himself was never angry for his own sake, only when the limits of Allah were violated — and even then he did not lose control.
Allah praised in the Qur'an "those who restrain anger and pardon people," and the Prophet ﷺ promised that whoever swallows his anger when he could act on it will be called by Allah on the Day of Judgement to choose whichever of the maidens of Paradise he wishes.
## Practice
- Delay your response; anger passes like a storm if you do not feed it.
- Remove yourself from the situation, then return calm.
- Remember that the one who angered you is a test, and your restraint is the answer.

== Modesty
Haya is the character of Islam. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Every religion has a character, and the character of Islam is haya." It is the shyness before Allah that keeps a person from sin in private, and the dignity before people that keeps him from indecency in public.
> الحَيَاءُ لا يَأْتِي إِلاَّ بِخَيْرٍ.
~ Bukhari and Muslim — "Modesty brings nothing but good."
The Prophet ﷺ was more modest than a maiden in her chamber; when he disliked something it showed on his face rather than in harsh words. Yet his modesty never prevented him from teaching what people needed to know; and he praised the women of the Helpers because shyness did not stop them from asking about religion.
Haya shows in the way we dress, the way we speak, the way we lower our gaze, the way we behave when no one is watching, and the way we decline to expose our own sins after Allah has covered them. The Prophet ﷺ said that the whole of his nation is forgiven except those who sin openly — who spend the night covered by Allah and then expose themselves in the morning.
## Practice
- Dress and speak with dignity; modesty is beauty, not restriction.
- Guard your eyes; the gaze is an arrow of Shaytan.
- Do not broadcast your sins; repent between yourself and Allah.
- Be shy before Allah as you would be before a noble person who sees you at all times.
