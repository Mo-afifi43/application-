# The Forty Hadith of Imam al-Nawawi

== 1. Deeds are judged by intentions
> إِنَّمَا الأَعْمَالُ بِالنِّيَّاتِ، وَإِنَّمَا لِكُلِّ امْرِئٍ مَا نَوَى، فَمَنْ كَانَتْ هِجْرَتُهُ إِلَى اللهِ وَرَسُولِهِ فَهِجْرَتُهُ إِلَى اللهِ وَرَسُولِهِ، وَمَنْ كَانَتْ هِجْرَتُهُ لِدُنْيَا يُصِيبُهَا أَوِ امْرَأَةٍ يَنْكِحُهَا فَهِجْرَتُهُ إِلَى مَا هَاجَرَ إِلَيْهِ.
~ Narrated by Umar ibn al-Khattab · Bukhari and Muslim
> "Actions are judged only by intentions, and every person will have only what he intended. So whoever migrated for Allah and His Messenger, his migration is for Allah and His Messenger; and whoever migrated for some worldly gain or for a woman to marry, his migration is for whatever he migrated for."
Scholars said that this single hadith is a third of Islam, because everything a Muslim does passes through the heart before it reaches the hands. The same act of prayer, charity or study can be a ladder to Paradise or an empty habit, depending on the intention behind it.
Intention is not a phrase you say; it is the direction of the heart. Before you begin any deed, ask quietly: for whom am I doing this? When the answer is "for Allah", even eating, sleeping and working become worship.
! Practice: renew your intention at the start of each day. Even a small deed done sincerely for Allah outweighs a mountain of deeds done for show.

== 2. The Hadith of Jibril
> بَيْنَمَا نَحْنُ جُلُوسٌ عِنْدَ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ ذَاتَ يَوْمٍ إِذْ طَلَعَ عَلَيْنَا رَجُلٌ شَدِيدُ بَيَاضِ الثِّيَابِ، شَدِيدُ سَوَادِ الشَّعْرِ... فَقَالَ: يَا مُحَمَّدُ أَخْبِرْنِي عَنِ الإِسْلامِ. فَقَالَ: الإِسْلامُ أَنْ تَشْهَدَ أَنْ لا إِلَهَ إِلاَّ اللهُ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللهِ، وَتُقِيمَ الصَّلاةَ، وَتُؤْتِيَ الزَّكَاةَ، وَتَصُومَ رَمَضَانَ، وَتَحُجَّ البَيْتَ إِنِ اسْتَطَعْتَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلاً... قَالَ: فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنِ الإِيمَانِ. قَالَ: أَنْ تُؤْمِنَ بِاللهِ وَمَلائِكَتِهِ وَكُتُبِهِ وَرُسُلِهِ وَاليَوْمِ الآخِرِ، وَتُؤْمِنَ بِالقَدَرِ خَيْرِهِ وَشَرِّهِ... قَالَ: فَأَخْبِرْنِي عَنِ الإِحْسَانِ. قَالَ: أَنْ تَعْبُدَ اللهَ كَأَنَّكَ تَرَاهُ، فَإِنْ لَمْ تَكُنْ تَرَاهُ فَإِنَّهُ يَرَاكَ... ثُمَّ قَالَ: يَا عُمَرُ أَتَدْرِي مَنِ السَّائِلُ؟ قُلْتُ: اللهُ وَرَسُولُهُ أَعْلَمُ. قَالَ: فَإِنَّهُ جِبْرِيلُ أَتَاكُمْ يُعَلِّمُكُمْ دِينَكُمْ.
~ Narrated by Umar ibn al-Khattab · Muslim
> "While we were sitting with the Messenger of Allah ﷺ one day, a man came to us whose clothes were exceedingly white and whose hair was exceedingly black... He said: O Muhammad, tell me about Islam. He ﷺ said: Islam is to testify that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, to establish the prayer, to give zakat, to fast Ramadan, and to make pilgrimage to the House if you are able. He said: Tell me about faith. He said: To believe in Allah, His angels, His books, His messengers, the Last Day, and to believe in the divine decree, its good and its bad. He said: Tell me about Ihsan. He said: To worship Allah as though you see Him, and if you do not see Him, He surely sees you... Then he said: O Umar, do you know who the questioner was? I said: Allah and His Messenger know best. He said: It was Jibril, who came to teach you your religion."
This hadith is called "the mother of the Sunnah" because it lays out the whole religion in three levels: Islam (what the limbs do), Iman (what the heart believes) and Ihsan (the excellence of worshipping as if you can see Allah).
Notice how the angel came as a stranger, sat close to the Prophet ﷺ with the manners of a student, and confirmed each answer. The companions learned that questions are the keys to knowledge, and that religion is not only rules but also a living awareness of Allah.
- Islam: the five pillars of action.
- Iman: the six pillars of belief.
- Ihsan: the summit — worship with presence of heart.

== 3. Islam is built on five
> بُنِيَ الإِسْلامُ عَلَى خَمْسٍ: شَهَادَةِ أَنْ لا إِلَهَ إِلاَّ اللهُ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللهِ، وَإِقَامِ الصَّلاةِ، وَإِيتَاءِ الزَّكَاةِ، وَحَجِّ البَيْتِ، وَصَوْمِ رَمَضَانَ.
~ Narrated by Abdullah ibn Umar · Bukhari and Muslim
> "Islam is built upon five: the testimony that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establishing the prayer, giving zakat, pilgrimage to the House, and fasting Ramadan."
The Prophet ﷺ compared Islam to a building resting on five pillars. A house without pillars collapses; a Muslim who neglects these pillars has a faith without structure.
The testimony of faith is the foundation. Prayer is the pillar that connects you to Allah five times a day. Zakat purifies wealth and cares for the poor. Fasting trains the soul, and Hajj gathers the whole nation in one place, dressed alike, calling on one Lord.
! The pillars are the minimum, not the maximum. Upon them the believer builds good character, service and knowledge.

== 4. The stages of creation and the decree
> إِنَّ أَحَدَكُمْ يُجْمَعُ خَلْقُهُ فِي بَطْنِ أُمِّهِ أَرْبَعِينَ يَوْمًا نُطْفَةً، ثُمَّ يَكُونُ عَلَقَةً مِثْلَ ذَلِكَ، ثُمَّ يَكُونُ مُضْغَةً مِثْلَ ذَلِكَ، ثُمَّ يُرْسَلُ إِلَيْهِ المَلَكُ فَيَنْفُخُ فِيهِ الرُّوحَ، وَيُؤْمَرُ بِأَرْبَعِ كَلِمَاتٍ: بِكَتْبِ رِزْقِهِ، وَأَجَلِهِ، وَعَمَلِهِ، وَشَقِيٌّ أَوْ سَعِيدٌ...
~ Narrated by Abdullah ibn Mas'ud · Bukhari and Muslim
> "Each of you is gathered in his mother's womb for forty days as a drop, then he is a clinging clot for a similar period, then a lump of flesh for a similar period. Then the angel is sent to him and breathes the soul into him, and is commanded to write four things: his provision, his lifespan, his deeds, and whether he will be wretched or blessed..."
This hadith opens a window onto the unseen. Long before a person takes his first breath, Allah's knowledge encompasses his whole story. Yet the hadith does not teach fatalism: the end of the narration warns that a person may do the deeds of the people of Paradise until only an arm's length remains, and then what was written overtakes him — a call to never feel secure in one's own deeds, and never to despair of Allah's mercy.
A believer therefore works hard, hopes in Allah, and asks constantly for a good ending.

== 5. Innovation in religion is rejected
> مَنْ أَحْدَثَ فِي أَمْرِنَا هَذَا مَا لَيْسَ مِنْهُ فَهُوَ رَدٌّ.
~ Narrated by Aisha · Bukhari and Muslim
> "Whoever introduces into this matter of ours something that is not part of it, it is rejected."
Religion is what Allah revealed, not what people invent. This hadith protects the purity of Islam: worship is defined by revelation, so no one has the right to add new acts of worship and attribute them to the religion.
It does not forbid new tools, technologies or organisations that help people live — those are worldly matters. What it forbids is inventing forms of worship that the Prophet ﷺ never taught, however good they may appear.
! The safest path is the path of the Prophet ﷺ and his companions.

== 6. The lawful is clear and the unlawful is clear
> إِنَّ الحَلالَ بَيِّنٌ وَإِنَّ الحَرَامَ بَيِّنٌ، وَبَيْنَهُمَا أُمُورٌ مُشْتَبِهَاتٌ لا يَعْلَمُهُنَّ كَثِيرٌ مِنَ النَّاسِ، فَمَنِ اتَّقَى الشُّبُهَاتِ فَقَدِ اسْتَبْرَأَ لِدِينِهِ وَعِرْضِهِ... أَلا وَإِنَّ فِي الجَسَدِ مُضْغَةً إِذَا صَلَحَتْ صَلَحَ الجَسَدُ كُلُّهُ، وَإِذَا فَسَدَتْ فَسَدَ الجَسَدُ كُلُّهُ، أَلا وَهِيَ القَلْبُ.
~ Narrated by al-Nu'man ibn Bashir · Bukhari and Muslim
> "The lawful is clear and the unlawful is clear, and between them are doubtful matters which many people do not know. Whoever avoids the doubtful matters has protected his religion and his honour... Indeed, in the body there is a piece of flesh: if it is sound, the whole body is sound, and if it is corrupt, the whole body is corrupt. Indeed, it is the heart."
The Prophet ﷺ gave the image of a shepherd grazing near a protected pasture: whoever grazes near the boundary will soon cross it. A believer keeps a safe distance from what is doubtful, because sins rarely begin with a clear violation — they begin with "it is probably fine".
The hadith ends with the heart, the king of the body. Fix the heart and the limbs follow.

== 7. Religion is sincere advice
> الدِّينُ النَّصِيحَةُ. قُلْنَا: لِمَنْ؟ قَالَ: لِلَّهِ، وَلِكِتَابِهِ، وَلِرَسُولِهِ، وَلأَئِمَّةِ المُسْلِمِينَ وَعَامَّتِهِمْ.
~ Narrated by Tamim al-Dari · Muslim
> "Religion is sincere advice. We said: To whom? He said: To Allah, to His Book, to His Messenger, to the leaders of the Muslims and to their common people."
Nasihah means wanting good for the one you advise, with no hidden motive. Sincerity to Allah is to believe in Him and obey Him; to His Book is to honour it, read it and act by it; to His Messenger is to love and follow him; to the leaders is to help them upon truth and remind them gently; to ordinary Muslims is to guide them, cover their faults and love for them what you love for yourself.
! Advice that humiliates is not nasihah. The Prophet ﷺ advised in private, with kindness.

== 8. The sanctity of the Muslim
> أُمِرْتُ أَنْ أُقَاتِلَ النَّاسَ حَتَّى يَشْهَدُوا أَنْ لا إِلَهَ إِلاَّ اللهُ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللهِ، وَيُقِيمُوا الصَّلاةَ، وَيُؤْتُوا الزَّكَاةَ، فَإِذَا فَعَلُوا ذَلِكَ عَصَمُوا مِنِّي دِمَاءَهُمْ وَأَمْوَالَهُمْ إِلاَّ بِحَقِّ الإِسْلامِ، وَحِسَابُهُمْ عَلَى اللهِ.
~ Narrated by Abdullah ibn Umar · Bukhari and Muslim
> "I have been commanded to fight the people until they testify that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, establish the prayer and give zakat. If they do that, their blood and wealth are protected from me except by the right of Islam, and their reckoning is with Allah."
The scholars explain that "the people" here refers to the hostile pagan Arabs who fought the message, within the rules of war that Islam laid down; the hadith is not a licence to attack peaceful people, and the Qur'an is explicit that there is no compulsion in religion.
Its central lesson is the immense sanctity of a Muslim's life and property: once someone enters Islam, no one may harm him, and his inner state is left to Allah alone. We judge by what is apparent; Allah judges the hearts.

== 9. Avoid what is forbidden, do what you can
> مَا نَهَيْتُكُمْ عَنْهُ فَاجْتَنِبُوهُ، وَمَا أَمَرْتُكُمْ بِهِ فَأْتُوا مِنْهُ مَا اسْتَطَعْتُمْ، فَإِنَّمَا أَهْلَكَ الَّذِينَ مِنْ قَبْلِكُمْ كَثْرَةُ مَسَائِلِهِمْ وَاخْتِلافُهُمْ عَلَى أَنْبِيَائِهِمْ.
~ Narrated by Abu Hurayrah · Bukhari and Muslim
> "What I have forbidden you, avoid; what I have commanded you, do as much of it as you can. Those before you were destroyed only by their excessive questioning and their disagreeing with their prophets."
This hadith is a master key of Islamic law. Prohibitions are to be avoided entirely, while commands are carried out according to ability — a sick person prays sitting, a traveller shortens the prayer, a poor person is excused from zakat and Hajj.
It also warns against turning religion into endless argument. Ask to learn and to act, not to complicate.

== 10. Allah is pure and accepts only what is pure
> إِنَّ اللهَ طَيِّبٌ لا يَقْبَلُ إِلاَّ طَيِّبًا... ثُمَّ ذَكَرَ الرَّجُلَ يُطِيلُ السَّفَرَ أَشْعَثَ أَغْبَرَ يَمُدُّ يَدَيْهِ إِلَى السَّمَاءِ: يَا رَبِّ، يَا رَبِّ، وَمَطْعَمُهُ حَرَامٌ، وَمَشْرَبُهُ حَرَامٌ، وَمَلْبَسُهُ حَرَامٌ، وَغُذِيَ بِالحَرَامِ، فَأَنَّى يُسْتَجَابُ لِذَلِكَ؟
~ Narrated by Abu Hurayrah · Muslim
> "Allah is pure and accepts only what is pure... Then he mentioned a man who travels far, dishevelled and dusty, raising his hands to the sky: O Lord, O Lord — while his food is unlawful, his drink is unlawful, his clothing is unlawful and he was nourished with the unlawful. How can he be answered?"
Earning lawfully is an act of worship, and unlawful earnings block the door of supplication. The man in the hadith has every outward sign of humility, yet his prayers struggle to rise because of what is in his stomach.
! Before asking why your prayers are not answered, review where your money comes from.

== 11. Leave what makes you doubt
> دَعْ مَا يَرِيبُكَ إِلَى مَا لا يَرِيبُكَ.
~ Narrated by al-Hasan ibn Ali · Tirmidhi and Nasa'i
> "Leave what makes you doubt for what does not make you doubt."
A believer's heart is a sensitive instrument. When something unsettles it, that unease is itself a sign. The Prophet ﷺ taught his grandson, while still a boy, to prefer the clear and calm path over the murky one.
This rule brings peace: truth brings tranquillity, while sin and doubt bring restlessness.

== 12. Leaving what does not concern you
> مِنْ حُسْنِ إِسْلامِ المَرْءِ تَرْكُهُ مَا لا يَعْنِيهِ.
~ Narrated by Abu Hurayrah · Tirmidhi
> "Part of the excellence of a person's Islam is leaving what does not concern him."
Much of life's harm comes from involvement in what is not ours: other people's affairs, idle talk, curiosity about private matters, and the endless scroll of things that add nothing to our faith or our work.
Whoever learns to leave what does not concern him gains time, peace and dignity — and protects his tongue from the sins of gossip and slander.

== 13. Loving for your brother what you love for yourself
> لا يُؤْمِنُ أَحَدُكُمْ حَتَّى يُحِبَّ لأَخِيهِ مَا يُحِبُّ لِنَفْسِهِ.
~ Narrated by Anas ibn Malik · Bukhari and Muslim
> "None of you truly believes until he loves for his brother what he loves for himself."
Faith is not complete while the heart is full of envy. The believer wants good for others — health, guidance, provision — as much as he wants it for himself, and he dislikes for them the harm he dislikes for himself.
This is the cure for envy and the foundation of a loving community. Try it practically: when you see a blessing with someone, ask Allah to bless him in it.

== 14. The sanctity of blood
> لا يَحِلُّ دَمُ امْرِئٍ مُسْلِمٍ يَشْهَدُ أَنْ لا إِلَهَ إِلاَّ اللهُ وَأَنِّي رَسُولُ اللهِ إِلاَّ بِإِحْدَى ثَلاثٍ: الثَّيِّبُ الزَّانِي، وَالنَّفْسُ بِالنَّفْسِ، وَالتَّارِكُ لِدِينِهِ المُفَارِقُ لِلْجَمَاعَةِ.
~ Narrated by Abdullah ibn Mas'ud · Bukhari and Muslim
> "The blood of a Muslim who testifies that there is no god but Allah and that I am the Messenger of Allah is not lawful except in one of three cases: the married adulterer, a life for a life, and the one who abandons his religion and separates from the community."
The default in Islam is that human life is sacred. The three exceptions are grave crimes judged only by a legitimate court after due process — never by individuals. The hadith therefore closes the door to vigilante violence and shows how narrow the exceptions are.

== 15. Speak good or remain silent
> مَنْ كَانَ يُؤْمِنُ بِاللهِ وَاليَوْمِ الآخِرِ فَلْيَقُلْ خَيْرًا أَوْ لِيَصْمُتْ، وَمَنْ كَانَ يُؤْمِنُ بِاللهِ وَاليَوْمِ الآخِرِ فَلْيُكْرِمْ جَارَهُ، وَمَنْ كَانَ يُؤْمِنُ بِاللهِ وَاليَوْمِ الآخِرِ فَلْيُكْرِمْ ضَيْفَهُ.
~ Narrated by Abu Hurayrah · Bukhari and Muslim
> "Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day, let him speak good or remain silent. Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day, let him honour his neighbour. Whoever believes in Allah and the Last Day, let him honour his guest."
Three signs of true faith: a guarded tongue, a kind neighbour and a generous host. Each is tied to belief in the Last Day, because the one who remembers that he will be asked about every word and every neighbour behaves differently.
! A simple rule for the tongue: if it is not good, it is not worth saying.

== 16. Do not get angry
> أَنَّ رَجُلاً قَالَ لِلنَّبِيِّ ﷺ: أَوْصِنِي. قَالَ: لا تَغْضَبْ. فَرَدَّدَ مِرَارًا، قَالَ: لا تَغْضَبْ.
~ Narrated by Abu Hurayrah · Bukhari
> "A man said to the Prophet ﷺ: Advise me. He said: Do not get angry. The man repeated his request several times, and he said: Do not get angry."
The Prophet ﷺ knew this man and knew what he needed. Anger is the door to violence, broken families and regret. The hadith does not deny the emotion — it commands us not to let it rule us: to stay silent, sit down, make wudu, seek refuge in Allah from Shaytan and walk away.
The strong person is not the wrestler but the one who controls himself when angry.

== 17. Excellence in everything
> إِنَّ اللهَ كَتَبَ الإِحْسَانَ عَلَى كُلِّ شَيْءٍ، فَإِذَا قَتَلْتُمْ فَأَحْسِنُوا القِتْلَةَ، وَإِذَا ذَبَحْتُمْ فَأَحْسِنُوا الذِّبْحَةَ، وَلْيُحِدَّ أَحَدُكُمْ شَفْرَتَهُ وَلْيُرِحْ ذَبِيحَتَهُ.
~ Narrated by Shaddad ibn Aws · Muslim
> "Allah has prescribed excellence in everything. So when you kill, kill well; and when you slaughter, slaughter well. Let each of you sharpen his blade and spare the animal suffering."
If Islam demands mercy even at the moment of slaughtering an animal, what about the rest of life? Ihsan — doing things beautifully and thoroughly — applies to prayer, work, family, speech and even the way we treat animals and the environment.

== 18. Fear Allah wherever you are
> اتَّقِ اللهَ حَيْثُمَا كُنْتَ، وَأَتْبِعِ السَّيِّئَةَ الحَسَنَةَ تَمْحُهَا، وَخَالِقِ النَّاسَ بِخُلُقٍ حَسَنٍ.
~ Narrated by Abu Dharr and Mu'adh ibn Jabal · Tirmidhi
> "Fear Allah wherever you are, follow a bad deed with a good deed and it will wipe it out, and treat people with good character."
A complete programme for life in three lines: your relationship with Allah (taqwa, in public and in private), your relationship with your own mistakes (repair them quickly with good deeds) and your relationship with people (good character).

== 19. Be mindful of Allah and He will protect you
> يَا غُلامُ، إِنِّي أُعَلِّمُكَ كَلِمَاتٍ: احْفَظِ اللهَ يَحْفَظْكَ، احْفَظِ اللهَ تَجِدْهُ تُجَاهَكَ، إِذَا سَأَلْتَ فَاسْأَلِ اللهَ، وَإِذَا اسْتَعَنْتَ فَاسْتَعِنْ بِاللهِ، وَاعْلَمْ أَنَّ الأُمَّةَ لَوِ اجْتَمَعَتْ عَلَى أَنْ يَنْفَعُوكَ بِشَيْءٍ لَمْ يَنْفَعُوكَ إِلاَّ بِشَيْءٍ قَدْ كَتَبَهُ اللهُ لَكَ... رُفِعَتِ الأَقْلامُ وَجَفَّتِ الصُّحُفُ.
~ Narrated by Abdullah ibn Abbas · Tirmidhi
> "Young man, I shall teach you some words: Be mindful of Allah and He will protect you. Be mindful of Allah and you will find Him before you. When you ask, ask Allah; when you seek help, seek help from Allah. Know that if the whole nation gathered to benefit you with something, they could benefit you only with what Allah has written for you... The pens have been lifted and the pages have dried."
The Prophet ﷺ spoke these words to a boy riding behind him, and they have carried millions of hearts since. They teach that guarding Allah's limits brings Allah's guarding, and that fear of people melts away when you know that no one can harm or help you except by His decree.
! Memorise this hadith. It is a shield against anxiety.

== 20. Modesty is from the first prophethood
> إِنَّ مِمَّا أَدْرَكَ النَّاسُ مِنْ كَلامِ النُّبُوَّةِ الأُولَى: إِذَا لَمْ تَسْتَحِ فَاصْنَعْ مَا شِئْتَ.
~ Narrated by Abu Mas'ud al-Badri · Bukhari
> "Among the words people received from the earliest prophethood: if you feel no shame, then do as you wish."
Haya — modesty and a sense of shame before Allah and people — is a restraint that every prophet taught. When it is lost, nothing holds a person back. The saying can also be read as encouragement: if what you intend is something you would not be ashamed of before Allah, go ahead and do it.

== 21. Say "I believe in Allah" and be steadfast
> قُلْتُ: يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ، قُلْ لِي فِي الإِسْلامِ قَوْلاً لا أَسْأَلُ عَنْهُ أَحَدًا غَيْرَكَ. قَالَ: قُلْ آمَنْتُ بِاللهِ ثُمَّ اسْتَقِمْ.
~ Narrated by Sufyan ibn Abdullah al-Thaqafi · Muslim
> "I said: O Messenger of Allah, tell me something about Islam that I will not need to ask anyone else about. He said: Say 'I believe in Allah', then be steadfast."
Faith is the beginning; steadfastness is the journey. Istiqamah means staying on the straight path day after day, without extremes and without giving up. The Qur'an promises that angels descend upon those who say "our Lord is Allah" and then stay steadfast, saying: do not fear and do not grieve.

== 22. The path to Paradise
> أَنَّ رَجُلاً سَأَلَ رَسُولَ اللهِ ﷺ فَقَالَ: أَرَأَيْتَ إِذَا صَلَّيْتُ المَكْتُوبَاتِ، وَصُمْتُ رَمَضَانَ، وَأَحْلَلْتُ الحَلالَ، وَحَرَّمْتُ الحَرَامَ، وَلَمْ أَزِدْ عَلَى ذَلِكَ شَيْئًا، أَأَدْخُلُ الجَنَّةَ؟ قَالَ: نَعَمْ.
~ Narrated by Jabir ibn Abdullah · Muslim
> "A man asked the Messenger of Allah ﷺ: If I pray the obligatory prayers, fast Ramadan, treat the lawful as lawful and the unlawful as unlawful, and add nothing to that, will I enter Paradise? He said: Yes."
Paradise is not reserved for the elite. The one who fulfils the obligations and keeps away from the prohibitions has a promise from the Prophet ﷺ. Extra deeds raise the ranks, but the door is opened by the basics done consistently.

== 23. Purity is half of faith
> الطُّهُورُ شَطْرُ الإِيمَانِ، وَالحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ تَمْلأُ المِيزَانَ، وَسُبْحَانَ اللهِ وَالحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ تَمْلآنِ مَا بَيْنَ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالأَرْضِ، وَالصَّلاةُ نُورٌ، وَالصَّدَقَةُ بُرْهَانٌ، وَالصَّبْرُ ضِيَاءٌ، وَالقُرْآنُ حُجَّةٌ لَكَ أَوْ عَلَيْكَ، كُلُّ النَّاسِ يَغْدُو فَبَائِعٌ نَفْسَهُ فَمُعْتِقُهَا أَوْ مُوبِقُهَا.
~ Narrated by Abu Malik al-Ash'ari · Muslim
> "Purity is half of faith. 'Praise be to Allah' fills the scale, and 'Glory be to Allah' and 'Praise be to Allah' fill what is between the heavens and the earth. Prayer is a light, charity is a proof, patience is a brightness, and the Qur'an is an argument for you or against you. Every person sets out in the morning and sells himself, either freeing himself or destroying himself."
Every sentence here is a treasure: the weight of a simple phrase of praise, the light that prayer places in the face and the grave, the proof charity gives of sincere faith, the brightness patience brings in dark times. And the closing image: every day you sell yourself — to Allah, and you are free, or to your desires, and you are lost.

== 24. O My servants, I have forbidden injustice for Myself
> يَا عِبَادِي إِنِّي حَرَّمْتُ الظُّلْمَ عَلَى نَفْسِي وَجَعَلْتُهُ بَيْنَكُمْ مُحَرَّمًا فَلا تَظَالَمُوا. يَا عِبَادِي كُلُّكُمْ ضَالٌّ إِلاَّ مَنْ هَدَيْتُهُ فَاسْتَهْدُونِي أَهْدِكُمْ... يَا عِبَادِي إِنَّكُمْ تُخْطِئُونَ بِاللَّيْلِ وَالنَّهَارِ وَأَنَا أَغْفِرُ الذُّنُوبَ جَمِيعًا فَاسْتَغْفِرُونِي أَغْفِرْ لَكُمْ...
~ A hadith qudsi narrated by Abu Dharr · Muslim
> "O My servants, I have forbidden injustice for Myself and made it forbidden among you, so do not wrong one another. O My servants, all of you are astray except the one I guide, so seek guidance from Me and I will guide you... O My servants, you sin by night and by day, and I forgive all sins, so seek My forgiveness and I will forgive you..."
This is one of the most beloved hadith qudsi. Allah speaks to His servants with a tenderness that melts the heart: He needs nothing from us, our obedience does not increase His kingdom and our sins do not decrease it, yet He invites us to ask for guidance, forgiveness, food and clothing — from Him alone.
! Read the full hadith in Sahih Muslim and let it be a conversation between you and your Lord.

== 25. The wealthy have taken the rewards
> أَنَّ نَاسًا مِنْ أَصْحَابِ رَسُولِ اللهِ ﷺ قَالُوا: يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ، ذَهَبَ أَهْلُ الدُّثُورِ بِالأُجُورِ... قَالَ: أَوَلَيْسَ قَدْ جَعَلَ اللهُ لَكُمْ مَا تَصَّدَّقُونَ؟ إِنَّ بِكُلِّ تَسْبِيحَةٍ صَدَقَةً، وَكُلِّ تَكْبِيرَةٍ صَدَقَةً... وَأَمْرٌ بِالمَعْرُوفِ صَدَقَةٌ، وَنَهْيٌ عَنْ مُنْكَرٍ صَدَقَةٌ...
~ Narrated by Abu Dharr · Muslim
> "Some companions said: O Messenger of Allah, the wealthy have taken all the rewards — they pray as we pray and fast as we fast, but they give charity from their surplus wealth. He said: Has Allah not given you things to give in charity? Every glorification is a charity, every 'Allah is Greatest' is a charity... enjoining good is a charity, forbidding evil is a charity..."
The poor companions envied the rich — not for their comfort but for their reward. The Prophet ﷺ opened for them a door that is open to everyone: remembrance of Allah, kindness, guiding to good, and even lawful intimacy with one's spouse, all counted as charity. No one is too poor to give.

== 26. Every joint owes a charity
> كُلُّ سُلامَى مِنَ النَّاسِ عَلَيْهِ صَدَقَةٌ، كُلَّ يَوْمٍ تَطْلُعُ فِيهِ الشَّمْسُ: تَعْدِلُ بَيْنَ اثْنَيْنِ صَدَقَةٌ، وَتُعِينُ الرَّجُلَ فِي دَابَّتِهِ فَتَحْمِلُهُ عَلَيْهَا أَوْ تَرْفَعُ لَهُ عَلَيْهَا مَتَاعَهُ صَدَقَةٌ، وَالكَلِمَةُ الطَّيِّبَةُ صَدَقَةٌ، وَبِكُلِّ خُطْوَةٍ تَمْشِيهَا إِلَى الصَّلاةِ صَدَقَةٌ، وَتُمِيطُ الأَذَى عَنِ الطَّرِيقِ صَدَقَةٌ.
~ Narrated by Abu Hurayrah · Bukhari and Muslim
> "Every joint of a person owes a charity every day the sun rises: reconciling two people is a charity, helping a man onto his mount or lifting his baggage onto it is a charity, a kind word is a charity, every step you walk to the prayer is a charity, and removing something harmful from the road is a charity."
The human body has hundreds of joints, and each is a gift that calls for gratitude. The Prophet ﷺ made gratitude easy: kind words, small services, steps to the mosque and clearing a road. Gratitude becomes a lifestyle rather than an occasional feeling.

== 27. Righteousness is good character
> البِرُّ حُسْنُ الخُلُقِ، وَالإِثْمُ مَا حَاكَ فِي صَدْرِكَ وَكَرِهْتَ أَنْ يَطَّلِعَ عَلَيْهِ النَّاسُ.
~ Narrated by al-Nawwas ibn Sam'an · Muslim
> "Righteousness is good character, and sin is what wavers in your chest and you would dislike people to see."
Wabisah ibn Ma'bad came to ask about righteousness, and the Prophet ﷺ said: "Consult your heart." The heart of a believer has a moral compass: when something feels wrong and you would hide it from people, that is a warning, even if someone gives you a fatwa to do it.

== 28. Hold fast to the Sunnah
> وَعَظَنَا رَسُولُ اللهِ ﷺ مَوْعِظَةً وَجِلَتْ مِنْهَا القُلُوبُ وَذَرَفَتْ مِنْهَا العُيُونُ... فَقَالَ: أُوصِيكُمْ بِتَقْوَى اللهِ وَالسَّمْعِ وَالطَّاعَةِ وَإِنْ تَأَمَّرَ عَلَيْكُمْ عَبْدٌ، فَإِنَّهُ مَنْ يَعِشْ مِنْكُمْ فَسَيَرَى اخْتِلافًا كَثِيرًا، فَعَلَيْكُمْ بِسُنَّتِي وَسُنَّةِ الخُلَفَاءِ الرَّاشِدِينَ المَهْدِيِّينَ، عَضُّوا عَلَيْهَا بِالنَّوَاجِذِ، وَإِيَّاكُمْ وَمُحْدَثَاتِ الأُمُورِ، فَإِنَّ كُلَّ بِدْعَةٍ ضَلالَةٌ.
~ Narrated by al-Irbad ibn Sariyah · Abu Dawud and Tirmidhi
> "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ gave us an admonition that made hearts tremble and eyes flow with tears... He said: I advise you to fear Allah, and to hear and obey even if a slave is placed in authority over you. Whoever among you lives will see much disagreement, so hold on to my Sunnah and the Sunnah of the rightly guided caliphs; bite onto it with your molars. Beware of newly invented matters, for every innovation is misguidance."
The companions felt this was a farewell sermon, so they asked for advice — and received a compass for times of confusion: taqwa, unity, and clinging to the way of the Prophet ﷺ and his first successors.

== 29. The gates of goodness
> قُلْتُ: يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ، أَخْبِرْنِي بِعَمَلٍ يُدْخِلُنِي الجَنَّةَ وَيُبَاعِدُنِي عَنِ النَّارِ. قَالَ: لَقَدْ سَأَلْتَ عَنْ عَظِيمٍ، وَإِنَّهُ لَيَسِيرٌ عَلَى مَنْ يَسَّرَهُ اللهُ عَلَيْهِ: تَعْبُدُ اللهَ لا تُشْرِكُ بِهِ شَيْئًا، وَتُقِيمُ الصَّلاةَ، وَتُؤْتِي الزَّكَاةَ، وَتَصُومُ رَمَضَانَ، وَتَحُجُّ البَيْتَ... ثُمَّ قَالَ: أَلا أَدُلُّكَ عَلَى أَبْوَابِ الخَيْرِ؟ الصَّوْمُ جُنَّةٌ، وَالصَّدَقَةُ تُطْفِئُ الخَطِيئَةَ كَمَا يُطْفِئُ المَاءُ النَّارَ، وَصَلاةُ الرَّجُلِ فِي جَوْفِ اللَّيْلِ...
~ Narrated by Mu'adh ibn Jabal · Tirmidhi
> "I said: O Messenger of Allah, tell me of a deed that will admit me to Paradise and distance me from the Fire. He said: You have asked about something great, yet it is easy for the one Allah makes it easy for: worship Allah without associating anything with Him, establish the prayer, give zakat, fast Ramadan and make pilgrimage... Then he said: Shall I not guide you to the gates of goodness? Fasting is a shield, charity extinguishes sin as water extinguishes fire, and a man's prayer in the depth of the night..."
The hadith continues: the head of the matter is Islam, its pillar is prayer, and its peak is striving in Allah's cause; and it ends with the Prophet ﷺ holding his tongue and saying: "Restrain this." Most of what throws people into the Fire on their faces is the harvest of their tongues.

== 30. Allah's limits
> إِنَّ اللهَ فَرَضَ فَرَائِضَ فَلا تُضَيِّعُوهَا، وَحَدَّ حُدُودًا فَلا تَعْتَدُوهَا، وَحَرَّمَ أَشْيَاءَ فَلا تَنْتَهِكُوهَا، وَسَكَتَ عَنْ أَشْيَاءَ رَحْمَةً لَكُمْ غَيْرَ نِسْيَانٍ فَلا تَبْحَثُوا عَنْهَا.
~ Narrated by Abu Tha'labah al-Khushani · al-Daraqutni
> "Allah has laid down obligations, so do not neglect them; He has set limits, so do not transgress them; He has forbidden things, so do not violate them; and He has remained silent about things out of mercy for you, not forgetfulness, so do not search into them."
A wise summary of the law: obligations, limits, prohibitions and a wide space of permissibility that Allah left open deliberately. Religion is easier than many people make it.

== 31. True asceticism
> جَاءَ رَجُلٌ إِلَى النَّبِيِّ ﷺ فَقَالَ: يَا رَسُولَ اللهِ، دُلَّنِي عَلَى عَمَلٍ إِذَا عَمِلْتُهُ أَحَبَّنِي اللهُ وَأَحَبَّنِي النَّاسُ. فَقَالَ: ازْهَدْ فِي الدُّنْيَا يُحِبَّكَ اللهُ، وَازْهَدْ فِيمَا عِنْدَ النَّاسِ يُحِبَّكَ النَّاسُ.
~ Narrated by Sahl ibn Sa'd · Ibn Majah
> "A man came to the Prophet ﷺ and said: O Messenger of Allah, guide me to a deed which, if I do it, Allah will love me and people will love me. He said: Be detached from the world and Allah will love you; be detached from what people possess and people will love you."
Zuhd is not poverty; it is freedom of the heart from the world. The one who does not chase what people have is loved by them, because he never competes with them or envies them.

== 32. No harm and no reciprocating harm
> لا ضَرَرَ وَلا ضِرَارَ.
~ Narrated by Abu Sa'id al-Khudri · Ibn Majah and al-Daraqutni
> "There should be neither harm nor reciprocating harm."
Four words that jurists built entire chapters upon. A Muslim does not harm others, and does not answer harm with harm beyond justice. This principle governs neighbours, trade, marriage, the environment and public life.

== 33. The burden of proof
> لَوْ يُعْطَى النَّاسُ بِدَعْوَاهُمْ لادَّعَى رِجَالٌ أَمْوَالَ قَوْمٍ وَدِمَاءَهُمْ، لَكِنِ البَيِّنَةُ عَلَى المُدَّعِي وَاليَمِينُ عَلَى مَنْ أَنْكَرَ.
~ Narrated by Abdullah ibn Abbas · al-Bayhaqi; part of it in Bukhari and Muslim
> "If people were given what they claimed, men would claim the wealth and blood of others. But the burden of proof is on the claimant, and the oath is on the one who denies."
Justice needs rules. Islam protects people from false accusations by demanding evidence from the one who claims, and it protects the innocent by accepting their oath when there is no proof against them.

== 34. Changing wrong
> مَنْ رَأَى مِنْكُمْ مُنْكَرًا فَلْيُغَيِّرْهُ بِيَدِهِ، فَإِنْ لَمْ يَسْتَطِعْ فَبِلِسَانِهِ، فَإِنْ لَمْ يَسْتَطِعْ فَبِقَلْبِهِ، وَذَلِكَ أَضْعَفُ الإِيمَانِ.
~ Narrated by Abu Sa'id al-Khudri · Muslim
> "Whoever among you sees a wrong, let him change it with his hand; if he cannot, then with his tongue; and if he cannot, then with his heart — and that is the weakest of faith."
Believers are not indifferent to wrong. Yet change has an order: with the hand for those with authority (a parent at home, a ruler in the state), with the tongue through wise advice, and with the heart by hating the wrong and refusing to take part in it.

== 35. The brotherhood of Muslims
> لا تَحَاسَدُوا، وَلا تَنَاجَشُوا، وَلا تَبَاغَضُوا، وَلا تَدَابَرُوا، وَلا يَبِعْ بَعْضُكُمْ عَلَى بَيْعِ بَعْضٍ، وَكُونُوا عِبَادَ اللهِ إِخْوَانًا. المُسْلِمُ أَخُو المُسْلِمِ: لا يَظْلِمُهُ، وَلا يَخْذُلُهُ، وَلا يَكْذِبُهُ، وَلا يَحْقِرُهُ. التَّقْوَى هَاهُنَا — وَيُشِيرُ إِلَى صَدْرِهِ ثَلاثَ مَرَّاتٍ — بِحَسْبِ امْرِئٍ مِنَ الشَّرِّ أَنْ يَحْقِرَ أَخَاهُ المُسْلِمَ، كُلُّ المُسْلِمِ عَلَى المُسْلِمِ حَرَامٌ: دَمُهُ وَمَالُهُ وَعِرْضُهُ.
~ Narrated by Abu Hurayrah · Muslim
> "Do not envy one another, do not inflate prices against one another, do not hate one another, do not turn away from one another, and do not undercut one another's sales; be servants of Allah, brothers. A Muslim is the brother of a Muslim: he does not wrong him, abandon him, lie to him or despise him. Taqwa is here — and he pointed to his chest three times. It is enough evil for a person to despise his Muslim brother. All of a Muslim is sacred to another Muslim: his blood, his wealth and his honour."
A charter for community life. Notice that it forbids not only injustice but also contempt — the quiet arrogance that looks down on others.

== 36. Relieving hardship
> مَنْ نَفَّسَ عَنْ مُؤْمِنٍ كُرْبَةً مِنْ كُرَبِ الدُّنْيَا نَفَّسَ اللهُ عَنْهُ كُرْبَةً مِنْ كُرَبِ يَوْمِ القِيَامَةِ، وَمَنْ يَسَّرَ عَلَى مُعْسِرٍ يَسَّرَ اللهُ عَلَيْهِ فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالآخِرَةِ، وَمَنْ سَتَرَ مُسْلِمًا سَتَرَهُ اللهُ فِي الدُّنْيَا وَالآخِرَةِ، وَاللهُ فِي عَوْنِ العَبْدِ مَا كَانَ العَبْدُ فِي عَوْنِ أَخِيهِ، وَمَنْ سَلَكَ طَرِيقًا يَلْتَمِسُ فِيهِ عِلْمًا سَهَّلَ اللهُ لَهُ بِهِ طَرِيقًا إِلَى الجَنَّةِ...
~ Narrated by Abu Hurayrah · Muslim
> "Whoever relieves a believer of a hardship of this world, Allah will relieve him of a hardship on the Day of Resurrection. Whoever eases the difficulty of one in debt, Allah will ease things for him in this world and the next. Whoever conceals the fault of a Muslim, Allah will conceal his faults in this world and the next. Allah helps His servant as long as the servant helps his brother. Whoever treads a path seeking knowledge, Allah makes easy for him a path to Paradise..."
The reward matches the deed: relief for relief, ease for ease, covering for covering. The hadith ends with gatherings of Qur'an and knowledge, upon which tranquillity descends and mercy covers, and the sobering line: "whoever is slowed by his deeds will not be hastened by his lineage."

== 37. The generosity of Allah in recording deeds
> إِنَّ اللهَ كَتَبَ الحَسَنَاتِ وَالسَّيِّئَاتِ ثُمَّ بَيَّنَ ذَلِكَ، فَمَنْ هَمَّ بِحَسَنَةٍ فَلَمْ يَعْمَلْهَا كَتَبَهَا اللهُ عِنْدَهُ حَسَنَةً كَامِلَةً، وَإِنْ هَمَّ بِهَا فَعَمِلَهَا كَتَبَهَا اللهُ عِنْدَهُ عَشْرَ حَسَنَاتٍ إِلَى سَبْعِمِائَةِ ضِعْفٍ إِلَى أَضْعَافٍ كَثِيرَةٍ، وَإِنْ هَمَّ بِسَيِّئَةٍ فَلَمْ يَعْمَلْهَا كَتَبَهَا اللهُ عِنْدَهُ حَسَنَةً كَامِلَةً، وَإِنْ هَمَّ بِهَا فَعَمِلَهَا كَتَبَهَا اللهُ سَيِّئَةً وَاحِدَةً.
~ Narrated by Abdullah ibn Abbas · Bukhari and Muslim
> "Allah has recorded the good deeds and the bad deeds, then explained it: whoever intends a good deed and does not do it, Allah records it as a complete good deed; if he intends it and does it, Allah records it as ten to seven hundred times over, and many times more. If he intends a bad deed and does not do it, Allah records it as a complete good deed; and if he intends it and does it, Allah records it as one bad deed."
Look at the mathematics of mercy: good is multiplied, evil is counted once, and even a good intention that never becomes action is rewarded. Whoever understands this hadith can never despair of Allah.

== 38. Drawing near to Allah
> إِنَّ اللهَ تَعَالَى قَالَ: مَنْ عَادَى لِي وَلِيًّا فَقَدْ آذَنْتُهُ بِالحَرْبِ، وَمَا تَقَرَّبَ إِلَيَّ عَبْدِي بِشَيْءٍ أَحَبَّ إِلَيَّ مِمَّا افْتَرَضْتُهُ عَلَيْهِ، وَمَا يَزَالُ عَبْدِي يَتَقَرَّبُ إِلَيَّ بِالنَّوَافِلِ حَتَّى أُحِبَّهُ، فَإِذَا أَحْبَبْتُهُ كُنْتُ سَمْعَهُ الَّذِي يَسْمَعُ بِهِ، وَبَصَرَهُ الَّذِي يُبْصِرُ بِهِ، وَيَدَهُ الَّتِي يَبْطِشُ بِهَا، وَرِجْلَهُ الَّتِي يَمْشِي بِهَا، وَإِنْ سَأَلَنِي لأُعْطِيَنَّهُ، وَلَئِنِ اسْتَعَاذَنِي لأُعِيذَنَّهُ.
~ A hadith qudsi narrated by Abu Hurayrah · Bukhari
> "Allah the Exalted says: Whoever shows enmity to a friend of Mine, I declare war upon him. My servant does not draw near to Me with anything more beloved to Me than what I have made obligatory upon him. And My servant keeps drawing near to Me with voluntary deeds until I love him; and when I love him, I am his hearing with which he hears, his sight with which he sees, his hand with which he strikes and his foot with which he walks. If he asks Me, I will surely give him, and if he seeks My protection, I will surely protect him."
The road to Allah's love: first the obligations, then the voluntary deeds, patiently, until Allah guides every sense and limb of His beloved servant. This is the meaning of "I am his hearing and his sight": he hears, sees and acts only in what pleases Allah.

== 39. Mistakes and forgetfulness are pardoned
> إِنَّ اللهَ تَجَاوَزَ لِي عَنْ أُمَّتِي الخَطَأَ وَالنِّسْيَانَ وَمَا اسْتُكْرِهُوا عَلَيْهِ.
~ Narrated by Abdullah ibn Abbas · Ibn Majah and al-Bayhaqi
> "Allah has pardoned for my sake my nation for mistakes, forgetfulness and what they are forced to do."
A mercy that lifts guilt from the honest mistake, the forgotten duty and the act done under compulsion. Islam holds people responsible for what they choose, not for what they could not help.

== 40. Be in this world as a stranger
> أَخَذَ رَسُولُ اللهِ ﷺ بِمَنْكِبِي فَقَالَ: كُنْ فِي الدُّنْيَا كَأَنَّكَ غَرِيبٌ أَوْ عَابِرُ سَبِيلٍ. وَكَانَ ابْنُ عُمَرَ يَقُولُ: إِذَا أَمْسَيْتَ فَلا تَنْتَظِرِ الصَّبَاحَ، وَإِذَا أَصْبَحْتَ فَلا تَنْتَظِرِ المَسَاءَ، وَخُذْ مِنْ صِحَّتِكَ لِمَرَضِكَ، وَمِنْ حَيَاتِكَ لِمَوْتِكَ.
~ Narrated by Abdullah ibn Umar · Bukhari
> "The Messenger of Allah ﷺ took me by the shoulder and said: Be in this world as though you were a stranger or a traveller passing through. Ibn Umar used to say: When evening comes, do not expect the morning; when morning comes, do not expect the evening. Take from your health for your sickness, and from your life for your death."
A traveller does not build a palace at a rest stop. He takes what he needs, treats people well and keeps his eyes on home. This is how a believer relates to the world: he uses it without being owned by it.

== 41. Desire must follow revelation
> لا يُؤْمِنُ أَحَدُكُمْ حَتَّى يَكُونَ هَوَاهُ تَبَعًا لِمَا جِئْتُ بِهِ.
~ Narrated by Abdullah ibn Amr · authentic, as Imam al-Nawawi stated
> "None of you truly believes until his desire follows what I have brought."
Complete faith is when what you love and hate aligns with what Allah and His Messenger love and hate. The believer does not bend the religion to fit his wishes; he trains his wishes to fit the religion.

== 42. The vastness of forgiveness
> قَالَ اللهُ تَعَالَى: يَا ابْنَ آدَمَ، إِنَّكَ مَا دَعَوْتَنِي وَرَجَوْتَنِي غَفَرْتُ لَكَ عَلَى مَا كَانَ مِنْكَ وَلا أُبَالِي. يَا ابْنَ آدَمَ، لَوْ بَلَغَتْ ذُنُوبُكَ عَنَانَ السَّمَاءِ ثُمَّ اسْتَغْفَرْتَنِي غَفَرْتُ لَكَ. يَا ابْنَ آدَمَ، إِنَّكَ لَوْ أَتَيْتَنِي بِقُرَابِ الأَرْضِ خَطَايَا ثُمَّ لَقِيتَنِي لا تُشْرِكُ بِي شَيْئًا لأَتَيْتُكَ بِقُرَابِهَا مَغْفِرَةً.
~ A hadith qudsi narrated by Anas ibn Malik · Tirmidhi
> "Allah the Exalted says: O son of Adam, as long as you call upon Me and hope in Me, I forgive you for whatever you have done, and I do not mind. O son of Adam, were your sins to reach the clouds of the sky and then you sought My forgiveness, I would forgive you. O son of Adam, were you to come to Me with sins nearly filling the earth and then meet Me without associating anything with Me, I would bring you forgiveness nearly filling it."
Imam al-Nawawi chose to end his collection with hope. Three doors of forgiveness: supplication with hope, seeking forgiveness, and pure monotheism. Whatever your past, the door is open as long as you are breathing.
