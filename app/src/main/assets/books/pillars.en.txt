# Pillars of Islam & Faith

== The Hadith of Jibril: a map of the religion
One day a stranger in brilliant white clothes sat before the Prophet ﷺ, knee to knee, and asked four questions: What is Islam? What is Iman? What is Ihsan? When is the Hour? When he left, the Prophet ﷺ told his companions: "That was Jibril; he came to teach you your religion."
That conversation is the map this book follows.
- Islam is the outward submission: five pillars of action.
- Iman is the inward conviction: six pillars of belief.
- Ihsan is the excellence of worshipping Allah as though you see Him.
> الإِسْلامُ أَنْ تَشْهَدَ أَنْ لا إِلَهَ إِلاَّ اللهُ وَأَنَّ مُحَمَّدًا رَسُولُ اللهِ، وَتُقِيمَ الصَّلاةَ، وَتُؤْتِيَ الزَّكَاةَ، وَتَصُومَ رَمَضَانَ، وَتَحُجَّ البَيْتَ إِنِ اسْتَطَعْتَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلاً
~ Sahih Muslim — "Islam is to testify that there is no god but Allah and that Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah, to establish the prayer, to give zakat, to fast Ramadan, and to make pilgrimage to the House if you are able."
The three levels are not separate religions but one religion at increasing depth. A person may perform the actions of Islam while his faith is still weak; as faith grows, the actions gain life; and when he reaches Ihsan, every act becomes an encounter with Allah.
! Knowing the pillars is not the same as living them. Read each chapter asking: how does this pillar shape my day?

== The testimony of faith
"La ilaha illa Allah, Muhammad rasul Allah" — there is no god but Allah, and Muhammad is the Messenger of Allah. These words are the gate of Islam. A person enters the religion by saying them with belief, and they are the first thing a Muslim wishes to say at the end of life.
The first half denies and affirms: it denies every false object of worship, and affirms that worship — love, fear, hope, prayer, sacrifice — belongs to Allah alone. This is Tawhid, the heart of the message of every prophet.
The second half commits you to a guide. To accept Muhammad ﷺ as the Messenger is to believe what he told us, obey what he commanded, avoid what he forbade, and worship Allah only in the way he taught.
> فَاعْلَمْ أَنَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا اللَّهُ
~ Qur'an 47:19 — "So know that there is no god but Allah."
The testimony has conditions that scholars gathered from the Qur'an and Sunnah: knowledge of its meaning, certainty, sincerity, truthfulness, love, submission and acceptance. It is not a formula recited once but a covenant renewed with every prayer.

== Prayer
Prayer is the pillar that holds the building up. It was made obligatory in the heavens on the night of the Mi'raj, five times a day, with the reward of fifty. It is the first thing a person will be asked about on the Day of Judgement.
> إِنَّ الصَّلَاةَ كَانَتْ عَلَى الْمُؤْمِنِينَ كِتَابًا مَّوْقُوتًا
~ Qur'an 4:103 — "Indeed, prayer has been decreed upon the believers at specified times."
Five daily appointments: Fajr before sunrise, Dhuhr after midday, Asr in the afternoon, Maghrib after sunset and Isha at night. Each one washes away the sins between it and the one before, like a river at the door in which a person bathes five times a day.
Prayer is more than movements. It is standing before the King, reciting His words, bowing and prostrating to Him. The Prophet ﷺ said that the closest a servant comes to his Lord is when he is in prostration, so make much supplication there.
- Pray on time; delaying without excuse drains the prayer of its light.
- Pray with presence; know what you are saying.
- Pray in congregation when you can; it multiplies the reward and binds the community.

== Zakat
Zakat is a fixed share of wealth, two and a half percent of savings that have reached a threshold and been held for a lunar year, given to the poor and other categories the Qur'an named. Its very name means purification and growth: it purifies the giver from greed and purifies wealth from the rights of others, and it grows the blessing in what remains.
> خُذْ مِنْ أَمْوَالِهِمْ صَدَقَةً تُطَهِّرُهُمْ وَتُزَكِّيهِم بِهَا
~ Qur'an 9:103 — "Take from their wealth a charity by which you purify them and cause them increase."
Zakat is not a favour to the poor; it is their right. Abu Bakr fought those who withheld it after the Prophet's death because it is a pillar, not an option. When it is paid correctly across a society, poverty has no place to hide.
Beyond the obligatory zakat, Islam encourages voluntary charity at every moment: a smile, a kind word, feeding a neighbour, planting a tree from which others eat. The hand that gives is better than the hand that takes, and charity never decreases wealth.

== Fasting
For one month each year, Muslims abstain from food, drink and intimacy from dawn to sunset. Ramadan is the month in which the Qur'an was revealed, and fasting in it is the fourth pillar.
> يَا أَيُّهَا الَّذِينَ آمَنُوا كُتِبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الصِّيَامُ كَمَا كُتِبَ عَلَى الَّذِينَ مِن قَبْلِكُمْ لَعَلَّكُمْ تَتَّقُونَ
~ Qur'an 2:183 — "O you who believe, fasting has been prescribed for you as it was prescribed for those before you, that you may become mindful of Allah."
The purpose is written in the verse: taqwa. Hunger teaches the rich what the poor feel; restraint teaches the soul that it can say no; and the empty stomach makes room for the Qur'an, for prayer at night and for generosity.
The Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever fasts Ramadan out of faith and seeking reward, his previous sins are forgiven." But he also warned that whoever does not give up false speech and evil deeds, Allah has no need of his giving up food and drink. Fasting of the stomach must become fasting of the tongue and the eyes.

== Hajj
Once in a lifetime, every Muslim who is physically and financially able must travel to Makkah in the month of Dhul-Hijjah and perform the rites that Ibrahim and Muhammad, peace be upon them, performed: circling the Ka'bah, walking between Safa and Marwah, standing on the plain of Arafat, and remembering Allah in Mina.
> وَلِلَّهِ عَلَى النَّاسِ حِجُّ الْبَيْتِ مَنِ اسْتَطَاعَ إِلَيْهِ سَبِيلًا
~ Qur'an 3:97 — "And pilgrimage to the House is a duty owed to Allah by those who are able to find a way."
Hajj is the pillar that gathers the whole nation: kings and labourers dressed in the same two white cloths, all calling "Labbayk Allahumma labbayk" — here I am, O Allah, here I am. It is a rehearsal for the Day of Resurrection and a return to the roots of Tawhid.
The Prophet ﷺ said that an accepted Hajj has no reward but Paradise, and that the pilgrim returns as free of sin as the day his mother bore him.

== Belief in Allah and His angels
The first pillar of faith is belief in Allah: that He exists, that He alone is the Creator, Provider and Owner of everything, that He alone deserves worship, and that He has the most beautiful names and perfect attributes, without likeness to any of His creation.
> لَيْسَ كَمِثْلِهِ شَيْءٌ ۖ وَهُوَ السَّمِيعُ الْبَصِيرُ
~ Qur'an 42:11 — "There is nothing like Him, and He is the All-Hearing, the All-Seeing."
The second pillar is belief in the angels: beings of light who never disobey Allah and do exactly what they are commanded. Jibril brings revelation, Mikail is entrusted with rain and provision, Israfil will blow the trumpet, the Angel of Death takes souls, and two recording angels sit with every person, writing every word.
Believing in angels changes behaviour. When you remember that noble writers are with you, you speak differently; when you remember that angels surround gatherings of remembrance, you seek those gatherings.

== Belief in the Books and the Messengers
Allah did not leave humanity without guidance. He sent scriptures: the Scrolls of Ibrahim, the Torah of Musa, the Zabur of Dawud, the Injil of Isa, and finally the Qur'an, which confirms them all and stands as guardian over them.
> آمَنَ الرَّسُولُ بِمَا أُنزِلَ إِلَيْهِ مِن رَّبِّهِ وَالْمُؤْمِنُونَ ۚ كُلٌّ آمَنَ بِاللَّهِ وَمَلَائِكَتِهِ وَكُتُبِهِ وَرُسُلِهِ
~ Qur'an 2:285 — "The Messenger has believed in what was revealed to him from his Lord, and so have the believers. All of them have believed in Allah, His angels, His books and His messengers."
A Muslim believes in every prophet, from Adam to Nuh, Ibrahim, Musa, Isa and Muhammad, peace be upon them all. They were human beings chosen by Allah, the best of people, who conveyed the message truthfully and were supported with miracles. They all called to one thing: worship Allah alone.
Muhammad ﷺ is the seal of the prophets. There is no prophet after him, and his message is for all people until the end of time.

== Belief in the Last Day
Life does not end at the grave. The believer knows that the trumpet will be blown, the dead will rise, the books of deeds will be opened, the scales will be set, and every person will be recompensed with perfect justice.
> فَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ خَيْرًا يَرَهُ ۝ وَمَن يَعْمَلْ مِثْقَالَ ذَرَّةٍ شَرًّا يَرَهُ
~ Qur'an 99:7–8 — "So whoever does an atom's weight of good will see it, and whoever does an atom's weight of evil will see it."
Belief in the Last Day includes the questioning in the grave, the resurrection, the gathering, the reckoning, the bridge over Hell, the pool of the Prophet ﷺ, and the two eternal abodes: Paradise for those who believed and did good, and the Fire for those who rejected the truth.
This belief is the engine of morality. The one who is certain of being asked does not oppress, does not cheat and does not despair, because he knows that every tear and every act of patience is counted.

== Divine decree, and the station of Ihsan
The sixth pillar is belief in Qadar: that Allah knew everything before it happened, wrote it in the Preserved Tablet, willed it, and created it. Nothing takes place in His kingdom except by His will and knowledge. Yet human beings have real choice and are responsible for it; they are commanded, forbidden, rewarded and punished according to what they choose.
> إِنَّا كُلَّ شَيْءٍ خَلَقْنَاهُ بِقَدَرٍ
~ Qur'an 54:49 — "Indeed, all things We created with a decree."
Belief in Qadar is the cure for anxiety and arrogance. What missed you could never have reached you, and what reached you could never have missed you. So the believer strives with his hands, trusts with his heart, and rests in whatever Allah chooses.
## Ihsan
Jibril's third question opened the highest station: "to worship Allah as though you see Him, and if you do not see Him, He surely sees you." Ihsan is presence. It turns prayer from ritual into meeting, and turns work, kindness and patience into acts done under the gaze of the Beloved.
! Begin small: in your next prayer, remind yourself before the first takbir that Allah is looking at you.
