# Stories of the Prophets

== Adam: the first man and the first repentance
Before the earth had a single human footprint, Allah told the angels: "I am placing a successor on the earth." He shaped Adam from clay with His own hands, breathed the soul into him, and taught him the names of all things — a knowledge the angels did not have. Then He commanded the angels to prostrate to Adam in honour, and they all did, except Iblis. He was of the jinn, and pride filled him: "I am better than him; You created me from fire and him from clay."
That refusal was the first sin in creation, and its root was arrogance.
Allah settled Adam and his wife Hawwa in Paradise with one command: do not approach this tree. Iblis, now an enemy, whispered to them that the tree would give them eternity. They ate, and their covering fell away, and they felt the weight of disobedience.
But here the story turns. Adam did not argue or blame as Iblis had done. He turned to his Lord with words that Allah Himself taught him:
> رَبَّنَا ظَلَمْنَا أَنفُسَنَا وَإِن لَّمْ تَغْفِرْ لَنَا وَتَرْحَمْنَا لَنَكُونَنَّ مِنَ الْخَاسِرِينَ
~ Qur'an 7:23 — "Our Lord, we have wronged ourselves, and if You do not forgive us and have mercy upon us, we will surely be among the losers."
Allah accepted his repentance and sent him down to the earth, not as a punishment alone but as the beginning of the human story: a life of test, with guidance from Allah and the promise that whoever follows it will have no fear and no grief.
## Lessons
- Pride ruined Iblis; humility saved Adam. The difference between them was not the sin but the response to it.
- Knowledge is honour: Adam was raised above the angels by what Allah taught him.
- Every child of Adam sins; the best of those who sin are those who repent.

== Nuh: nine hundred and fifty years of patience
Generations after Adam, people began to honour righteous men so much that they made statues of them, and then their descendants worshipped the statues. Allah sent Nuh to call them back to worshipping Him alone.
Nuh called them by night and by day, in public and in private. He reminded them of the rain, the crops, the children — the gifts of the One they had forgotten. They put their fingers in their ears, wrapped themselves in their garments and mocked him: only the poor and the weak follow you.
He continued for nine hundred and fifty years. Only a few believed. Finally Allah revealed that no one else would believe, and commanded him to build a ship on dry land. The people laughed as they passed by the carpenter-prophet. He answered: "If you mock us, we will mock you as you mock us; soon you will know."
Then the sky opened and the earth gushed water. Nuh boarded the ship with the believers and a pair of every animal. He called his son, who had stayed with the disbelievers: "My son, board with us!" The boy replied that he would climb a mountain. "There is no protector today from the command of Allah," said Nuh, and a wave came between them.
The ship came to rest on Mount Judi. Nuh grieved for his son, and Allah taught him that family is not blood alone: "He is not of your family; his deed was not righteous."
## Lessons
- Results belong to Allah; effort belongs to us. Nuh's success was not measured in numbers.
- The most painful trials come from those closest to us; faith must still come first.
- Whoever worships anything besides Allah has lost the purpose for which he was created.

== Hud and Salih: the fall of the mighty
The people of 'Ad lived in southern Arabia in a land of gardens and springs. They were tall and strong and built towering monuments on every hill, and they said: "Who is stronger than us?" Allah sent their brother Hud to remind them: the One who created you is stronger than you.
They called him a fool. He replied with the dignity of a prophet: "There is no foolishness in me; I am a messenger from the Lord of the worlds, and I am a sincere adviser to you." When they persisted, a cloud appeared and they rejoiced, thinking it was rain. It was a wind that raged for seven nights and eight days and left them like the hollow trunks of fallen palm trees.
After them came Thamud, who carved homes into the mountains with astonishing skill. Allah sent Salih from among them. They demanded a miracle, and Allah brought forth a she-camel from the rock. Salih warned them: let her drink and graze in peace, and do not touch her with harm. For a time they shared the water with her, one day for the camel and one day for them. Then the most wretched of them plotted and killed her.
Salih gave them three days. On the third day a mighty blast struck them and they were left lying lifeless in their homes.
## Lessons
- Power, technology and beautiful buildings do not protect a people from the consequences of arrogance and injustice.
- Allah's signs are a trust; those who harm them harm themselves.
- The prophets answered mockery with patience and clarity, never with insult.

== Ibrahim: the friend of Allah
Ibrahim grew up in a land of idol-makers; his own father carved statues. Even as a boy he could not accept that a piece of wood could be a god. The Qur'an tells how he looked at a star and said, "This is my lord," and when it set, he said, "I do not love what disappears." The moon rose and set, the sun rose and set, and he declared: "I turn my face to the One who created the heavens and the earth."
He argued gently with his father, and when his people left for a festival, he broke their idols with an axe and hung it on the largest one. "Ask them," he said when they confronted him, "if they can speak." They knew the idols were nothing, but pride prevailed, and they built a fire to burn him. Allah said: "O fire, be coolness and safety upon Ibrahim." He walked out unharmed.
Ibrahim left his homeland and travelled to Palestine, then Egypt, then back, worshipping Allah alone wherever he went. He was tested again and again — with childlessness until old age, with the command to leave his wife Hajar and infant son in a barren valley, and with the command to sacrifice his beloved son — and he passed every test. Allah called him His Khalil, His intimate friend, and made him the father of prophets.
> إِنَّ إِبْرَاهِيمَ كَانَ أُمَّةً قَانِتًا لِّلَّهِ حَنِيفًا وَلَمْ يَكُ مِنَ الْمُشْرِكِينَ
~ Qur'an 16:120 — "Indeed, Ibrahim was a nation in himself, devoutly obedient to Allah, inclining toward truth, and he was not of those who associated others with Allah."
## Lessons
- Truth can be found by reflecting honestly on creation.
- Loyalty to Allah comes before loyalty to family, tribe or tradition.
- Every hardship Ibrahim faced became a rank; trials are Allah's way of raising His friends.

== Ismail and Ishaq: the sacrifice and the House
Ibrahim brought Hajar and their baby Ismail to a valley with no water and no people, and turned to leave. Hajar ran after him: "Did Allah command you to do this?" He said yes. "Then He will not abandon us," she said. When the water ran out, she ran seven times between the hills of Safa and Marwah looking for help, and the angel struck the ground and the well of Zamzam sprang up at her child's feet. Pilgrims repeat her seven runs to this day.
Ismail grew up in that valley, and years later Ibrahim saw in a dream that he was sacrificing his son. A prophet's dream is revelation. He told Ismail, who answered: "Father, do what you are commanded; you will find me, if Allah wills, among the patient." As the knife was ready, Allah called out that the vision had been fulfilled and ransomed the boy with a great ram. Muslims remember this every Eid al-Adha.
Father and son then raised the foundations of the Ka'bah in Makkah, praying: "Our Lord, accept from us; You are the All-Hearing, the All-Knowing." From Ismail's descendants came the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ.
Allah also gave Ibrahim, in his old age, a son from Sarah: Ishaq, and after him Ya'qub, and from that line came the prophets of the Children of Israel.
## Lessons
- Hajar's certainty — "then He will not abandon us" — is the definition of tawakkul.
- Obedience that costs nothing is easy; Ibrahim and Ismail were tested with what they loved most.
- The greatest buildings on earth were built by hands that were busy with prayer.

== Yusuf: the most beautiful story
As a boy, Yusuf told his father Ya'qub that he had seen eleven stars, the sun and the moon prostrating to him. Ya'qub understood that a great future awaited his son, and warned him not to tell his brothers.
Jealousy had already taken root. The brothers threw Yusuf into a well, then told their father a wolf had eaten him. Ya'qub said only: "Patience is beautiful, and Allah is the one whose help I seek."
A caravan pulled Yusuf from the well and sold him in Egypt, where he grew up in the house of a minister. Handsome and trusted, he was tempted by the minister's wife, and he refused: "I seek refuge in Allah." When she accused him, he chose prison over sin.
In prison he called his fellow inmates to the worship of one God and interpreted their dreams. Years later, the king dreamed of seven fat cows eaten by seven lean ones, and only Yusuf could explain it: seven years of plenty followed by seven of famine. He was released, cleared of the accusation, and appointed over the storehouses of Egypt.
When the famine reached Canaan, his brothers came to buy grain and did not recognise him. He tested them, brought his younger brother to safety, and finally revealed himself. They were ashamed. He said the words that echo through history:
> لَا تَثْرِيبَ عَلَيْكُمُ الْيَوْمَ ۖ يَغْفِرُ اللَّهُ لَكُمْ
~ Qur'an 12:92 — "No blame upon you today. Allah will forgive you."
His family came to Egypt, and they fell in prostration before him: the dream fulfilled.
## Lessons
- Every stage that looked like a fall — the well, slavery, prison — was a step toward the throne.
- Chastity in private is the mark of a true servant.
- The one who has the power to punish and forgives instead is closest to the character of the prophets.

== Ayyub: the patient servant
Ayyub was a prophet blessed with wealth, land, cattle and many children, and he was grateful for all of it. Then Allah tested him. His wealth was lost, his children died, and a disease spread over his body until only his heart and tongue remained sound — and he used them to remember Allah.
Years passed. People turned away from him. His wife alone stayed, working to feed him. Through it all, he never complained about his Lord to anyone. When he finally spoke, it was a prayer that combined complaint to Allah with perfect manners toward Him:
> وَأَيُّوبَ إِذْ نَادَىٰ رَبَّهُ أَنِّي مَسَّنِيَ الضُّرُّ وَأَنتَ أَرْحَمُ الرَّاحِمِينَ
~ Qur'an 21:83 — "And Ayyub, when he called to his Lord: Indeed, harm has touched me, and You are the Most Merciful of the merciful."
Allah told him to strike the ground with his foot; a spring burst out, and he washed and drank and was healed. Allah restored his family and doubled his blessings, "as a mercy from Us and a reminder for the worshippers."
## Lessons
- Patience does not mean silence toward Allah; it means not complaining about Allah.
- Ayyub did not even ask directly for healing; he described his state and praised his Lord. Allah understood.
- Trials strip away everything except what really matters, and then Allah gives it all back.

== Musa: the one Allah spoke to
Pharaoh of Egypt enslaved the Children of Israel and ordered their newborn sons killed. Musa's mother placed him in a basket on the Nile, and the river carried him to Pharaoh's own palace, where Pharaoh's wife Asiya loved him and raised him. Allah returned him to his mother to nurse him, fulfilling His promise.
As a young man, Musa struck an Egyptian who was oppressing an Israelite, and the man died. He fled to Madyan, where he helped two young women water their flock, married one of them and worked for her father for ten years as a shepherd.
Returning with his family, he saw a fire on Mount Sinai. There Allah spoke to him directly, turned his staff into a serpent, made his hand shine white, and sent him with his brother Harun to Pharaoh: "Speak to him gently; perhaps he will be reminded or fear."
Pharaoh refused and gathered his magicians. When they threw their ropes, Musa threw his staff and it swallowed their illusions, and the magicians fell in prostration, believing at once. Plagues followed — locusts, lice, frogs, blood — and finally Allah commanded Musa to leave by night. Pharaoh's army caught up with them at the sea. "We are caught!" the people cried. Musa replied: "No. My Lord is with me; He will guide me." He struck the sea and it split into walls of water, the believers crossed, and Pharaoh drowned.
On the mountain, Allah gave Musa the Torah. His story fills the Qur'an more than any other, because it is the story of every struggle between faith and tyranny.
## Lessons
- What Pharaoh planned to destroy, Allah raised in Pharaoh's own house.
- "My Lord is with me" is the answer to every impossible situation.
- The prophet who could split the sea was told to speak gently — even to a tyrant.

== Dawud and Sulayman: kings who were servants
Dawud was a young man in the army of Talut when the giant Jalut challenged the believers. With a sling and complete trust in Allah, Dawud killed him, and Allah gave him kingship and prophethood together. He was given the Zabur, and a voice so beautiful that the mountains and birds echoed his praise. Allah softened iron for him, and he made armour with his hands and ate from his own work; the Prophet ﷺ said no one eats better food than what he earns himself, and that Dawud used to eat from the work of his hands.
His son Sulayman inherited the kingdom and asked Allah for a dominion no one after him would have. Allah made the wind obey him, the jinn work for him and taught him the speech of birds. Marching with his army, he heard an ant warn her colony to enter their homes, and he smiled and thanked Allah for the blessings he had been given.
The hoopoe brought news of a queen in Saba', Bilqis, whose people worshipped the sun. Sulayman sent her a letter beginning with "In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Especially Merciful," inviting her to submit. She came to his court, saw her own throne brought there in the blink of an eye, walked over a floor of crystal she mistook for water, and declared: "My Lord, I have wronged myself, and I submit with Sulayman to Allah, Lord of the worlds."
## Lessons
- Kingship is a test like poverty; Dawud and Sulayman used theirs for worship and justice.
- Gratitude was Sulayman's response to every blessing, from a talking ant to a queen's surrender.
- The greatest king in history began his letter with Bismillah.

== Yunus: the prayer from the depths
Yunus was sent to the people of Nineveh, a great city. They rejected him, and he grew frustrated and left them without waiting for Allah's permission, warning that punishment was near.
He boarded a ship. A storm rose, and the sailors cast lots to lighten the load; the lot fell on Yunus three times. He was thrown into the sea, and a great whale swallowed him. In the darkness of the belly of the whale, the darkness of the sea and the darkness of the night, he called out:
> لَّا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا أَنتَ سُبْحَانَكَ إِنِّي كُنتُ مِنَ الظَّالِمِينَ
~ Qur'an 21:87 — "There is no god but You; glory be to You; indeed, I have been of the wrongdoers."
Allah heard him and commanded the whale to cast him onto the shore, ill and weak, and grew a gourd plant to shade him. When he recovered, he returned to his people — and found that they had believed while he was gone. When they saw the signs of punishment approaching, the whole city had gone out in humility, and Allah had lifted it from them. They were the only people in the Qur'an whom belief saved as a whole nation.
The Prophet ﷺ said: "No Muslim calls upon Allah with the supplication of Yunus about any matter except that Allah answers him."
## Lessons
- Even a prophet needed patience; leaving before the appointed time led to the belly of the whale.
- The prayer of Yunus is the key of the distressed: monotheism, glorification and admission of fault.
- A whole people can turn to Allah at once — never lose hope in anyone.

== Zakariyya and Yahya: an answered prayer
Zakariyya was an elderly prophet who cared for the young Maryam in the temple of Jerusalem. Whenever he entered her chamber, he found provisions with her that no one had brought. "Where is this from?" he asked. "It is from Allah," she replied; "Allah provides for whom He wills without measure."
Her answer stirred something in the old man. He had no child, his wife was barren, his bones were weak and his hair white — but if Allah provides without measure, why not ask? There and then he prayed in a low voice:
> رَبِّ لَا تَذَرْنِي فَرْدًا وَأَنتَ خَيْرُ الْوَارِثِينَ
~ Qur'an 21:89 — "My Lord, do not leave me alone, while You are the best of inheritors."
The angels called to him while he stood praying: Allah gives you glad tidings of a son named Yahya, a name no one had been given before. The sign was that he would not speak to people for three days except by gesture.
Yahya was given wisdom while still a boy, and grew up gentle, pure, devoted to his parents and to his Lord. He called people to repentance, and he stood firmly for truth before a corrupt king, and was killed for it. Allah honoured him with peace "the day he was born, the day he dies and the day he is raised alive."
## Lessons
- Never let age, circumstances or statistics stop you from asking Allah.
- The best supplications are quiet and honest.
- A single righteous child is a legacy greater than any wealth.

== Isa: a word from Allah
Maryam, daughter of Imran, was raised in worship and purity. One day the angel Jibril appeared to her in the form of a man and announced that she would bear a pure son. "How can I have a son when no man has touched me?" she asked. He replied that this was easy for Allah: a sign for people and a mercy.
She withdrew to a distant place, and when the pains of birth drove her to the trunk of a palm tree, Allah comforted her with a stream at her feet and ripe dates falling upon her. She returned to her people carrying the child, and when they accused her, she pointed to the baby — and Isa spoke from the cradle: "I am the servant of Allah. He has given me the Scripture and made me a prophet."
Isa grew up and was sent to the Children of Israel with the Injil. By Allah's permission he healed the blind and the leper, gave life to the dead and shaped a bird from clay that flew. He called to the worship of Allah alone and to mercy, humility and detachment from the world. A small group of disciples, the Hawariyyun, supported him.
When his enemies plotted to kill him, Allah did not let them succeed. It was made to appear so to them, but Allah raised Isa to Himself. Muslims believe he will return before the end of time, and that he is a servant and messenger of Allah, born of a virgin, honoured above most of creation — but not divine.
> إِنَّ مَثَلَ عِيسَىٰ عِندَ اللَّهِ كَمَثَلِ آدَمَ ۖ خَلَقَهُ مِن تُرَابٍ ثُمَّ قَالَ لَهُ كُن فَيَكُونُ
~ Qur'an 3:59 — "Indeed, the example of Isa to Allah is like that of Adam. He created him from dust, then said to him: Be, and he was."
## Lessons
- Maryam is a model of purity and trust in Allah for every man and woman.
- Miracles are given by Allah; the prophets themselves were servants who prayed and fasted.
- Isa's message and Muhammad's message are one: worship Allah alone.
