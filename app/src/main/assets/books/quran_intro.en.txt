# Understanding the Qur'an

== What is the Qur'an?
The Qur'an is the speech of Allah, revealed to the Prophet Muhammad ﷺ through the angel Jibril over twenty-three years. It is the final message to humanity, confirming the truth that earlier prophets brought and protecting it from being lost or changed.
> إِنَّا نَحْنُ نَزَّلْنَا الذِّكْرَ وَإِنَّا لَهُ لَحَافِظُونَ
~ Qur'an 15:9 — "Indeed, it is We who sent down the Reminder, and indeed We are its Guardian."
Muslims do not treat the Qur'an as a book about Allah; it is a book from Allah. That is why its words are recited in prayer exactly as they were revealed, why children memorise it from cover to cover, and why a single verse can change the direction of a life.
The Qur'an has 114 surahs (chapters) and more than six thousand ayat (verses). Its themes are constant: the oneness of Allah, the stories of the prophets, the Hereafter, guidance for worship and society, and the beauty of creation as a sign of its Creator.
! A good habit: read a few verses every day with their meaning. The Qur'an was revealed gradually so that hearts could absorb it slowly.

== How the Qur'an was revealed
The first verses came down in the cave of Hira, near Makkah, when the Prophet ﷺ was forty years old. The angel embraced him and said: "Read!" He replied that he could not read. The command was repeated until the words were placed in his heart:
> اقْرَأْ بِاسْمِ رَبِّكَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ
~ Qur'an 96:1 — "Read in the name of your Lord who created."
From that night until his final days, revelation came in pieces, sometimes a few verses, sometimes a whole surah, often in answer to a question or an event. Verses revealed in Makkah focus on faith, the Hereafter and patience under persecution; verses revealed in Madinah organise the new community with laws of worship, family, trade and justice.
The Prophet ﷺ would recite each new passage to his companions and dictate it to the scribes of revelation. Jibril reviewed the whole Qur'an with him every Ramadan, and twice in the year he passed away.
- Revelation came in the Prophet's own language, Arabic, in a style no poet could match.
- It came gradually, so it could be memorised, understood and lived.
- It came in response to real situations, which is why knowing the context of a verse deepens its meaning.

== How the Qur'an was preserved
Two chains of preservation worked together from the very beginning: memory and writing. Hundreds of companions memorised the Qur'an completely, and the Prophet ﷺ had it written on parchment, leather and palm stalks by trusted scribes such as Zayd ibn Thabit.
After the Prophet's death, Abu Bakr ordered the written pieces to be gathered into one master copy, verified against the memory of the reciters. During the caliphate of Uthman, copies of that master were sent to the major cities so that the whole nation would recite from a single text.
The result is unique in human history: a scripture recited today, letter for letter, as it was recited fourteen centuries ago, by millions who carry it in their hearts in every country on earth.
! The chain has never been broken. Every teacher of Qur'an received it from a teacher, back to the Prophet ﷺ.

== The etiquette of reciting the Qur'an
Because the Qur'an is the speech of Allah, Muslims approach it with reverence:
- Be in a state of purity, ideally with wudu, and face the Qur'an with a calm heart.
- Begin with seeking refuge in Allah from Shaytan, then "Bismillah ar-Rahman ar-Rahim".
- Recite slowly and clearly (tartil), giving each letter its right.
- Reflect on the meaning; the Qur'an asks its readers again and again: "Will they not reflect?"
- Act upon what you read. The companions would learn ten verses and not move on until they had put them into practice.
- Handle the book with respect, and do not read it while others are talking loudly or distracted.
> وَرَتِّلِ الْقُرْآنَ تَرْتِيلًا
~ Qur'an 73:4 — "And recite the Qur'an with measured recitation."
The Prophet ﷺ said that the one who recites the Qur'an skilfully is with the noble angels, and the one who struggles and stumbles has two rewards. So no one should be discouraged by a slow start.

== Surat al-Fatihah: the Opening
Al-Fatihah is the surah every Muslim recites in every unit of prayer, at least seventeen times a day. The Prophet ﷺ called it "the greatest surah in the Qur'an" and "the Mother of the Book".
> بِسْمِ اللَّهِ الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ
> الْحَمْدُ لِلَّهِ رَبِّ الْعَالَمِينَ
> الرَّحْمَٰنِ الرَّحِيمِ
> مَالِكِ يَوْمِ الدِّينِ
> إِيَّاكَ نَعْبُدُ وَإِيَّاكَ نَسْتَعِينُ
> اهْدِنَا الصِّرَاطَ الْمُسْتَقِيمَ
> صِرَاطَ الَّذِينَ أَنْعَمْتَ عَلَيْهِمْ غَيْرِ الْمَغْضُوبِ عَلَيْهِمْ وَلَا الضَّالِّينَ
~ Surat al-Fatihah
"In the name of Allah, the Most Merciful, the Especially Merciful. All praise is for Allah, Lord of all the worlds, the Most Merciful, the Especially Merciful, Master of the Day of Judgement. You alone we worship and You alone we ask for help. Guide us to the straight path — the path of those You have blessed, not of those who earned anger, nor of those who went astray."
The surah is a conversation. In a hadith qudsi, Allah says: "I have divided the prayer between Me and My servant in two halves." When the servant says "All praise is for Allah", Allah says: "My servant has praised Me." When he says "You alone we worship", Allah says: "This is between Me and My servant, and My servant shall have what he asked for."
Its structure teaches how to ask: begin with praise, acknowledge His mercy and His mastery, declare your servitude, then make the greatest request of all — guidance.

== Ayat al-Kursi: the Verse of the Throne
The Prophet ﷺ told Ubayy ibn Ka'b that this is the greatest verse in the Book of Allah. Whoever recites it after each obligatory prayer, nothing stands between him and Paradise except death; and whoever recites it before sleeping is protected by Allah until morning.
> اللَّهُ لَا إِلَٰهَ إِلَّا هُوَ الْحَيُّ الْقَيُّومُ ۚ لَا تَأْخُذُهُ سِنَةٌ وَلَا نَوْمٌ ۚ لَّهُ مَا فِي السَّمَاوَاتِ وَمَا فِي الْأَرْضِ ۗ مَن ذَا الَّذِي يَشْفَعُ عِندَهُ إِلَّا بِإِذْنِهِ ۚ يَعْلَمُ مَا بَيْنَ أَيْدِيهِمْ وَمَا خَلْفَهُمْ ۖ وَلَا يُحِيطُونَ بِشَيْءٍ مِّنْ عِلْمِهِ إِلَّا بِمَا شَاءَ ۚ وَسِعَ كُرْسِيُّهُ السَّمَاوَاتِ وَالْأَرْضَ ۖ وَلَا يَئُودُهُ حِفْظُهُمَا ۚ وَهُوَ الْعَلِيُّ الْعَظِيمُ
~ Qur'an 2:255
"Allah — there is no god but He, the Ever-Living, the Sustainer of all. Neither drowsiness nor sleep overtakes Him. To Him belongs whatever is in the heavens and whatever is on the earth. Who is it that can intercede with Him except by His permission? He knows what is before them and what is behind them, and they encompass nothing of His knowledge except what He wills. His Kursi extends over the heavens and the earth, and preserving them does not tire Him. And He is the Most High, the Most Great."
Every clause of the verse describes Allah's perfection: His life needs no sleep, His kingdom has no partner, His knowledge has no limit, His power has no fatigue. Recite it with understanding and it becomes a fortress for the heart.

== The three protecting surahs
The Prophet ﷺ used to recite Surat al-Ikhlas, al-Falaq and al-Nas every night, blowing into his palms and wiping over his body, and he taught that they are the best protection a person can seek.
## Surat al-Ikhlas
> قُلْ هُوَ اللَّهُ أَحَدٌ
> اللَّهُ الصَّمَدُ
> لَمْ يَلِدْ وَلَمْ يُولَدْ
> وَلَمْ يَكُن لَّهُ كُفُوًا أَحَدٌ
~ Surat al-Ikhlas — "Say: He is Allah, the One. Allah, the Eternal Refuge. He neither begets nor is born, and there is none comparable to Him."
The Prophet ﷺ said this surah equals a third of the Qur'an, because it contains the essence of belief in Allah.
## Surat al-Falaq
> قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ الْفَلَقِ
> مِن شَرِّ مَا خَلَقَ
> وَمِن شَرِّ غَاسِقٍ إِذَا وَقَبَ
> وَمِن شَرِّ النَّفَّاثَاتِ فِي الْعُقَدِ
> وَمِن شَرِّ حَاسِدٍ إِذَا حَسَدَ
~ Surat al-Falaq — "Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of the daybreak, from the evil of what He created, from the evil of darkness when it settles, from the evil of those who blow on knots, and from the evil of an envier when he envies."
## Surat al-Nas
> قُلْ أَعُوذُ بِرَبِّ النَّاسِ
> مَلِكِ النَّاسِ
> إِلَٰهِ النَّاسِ
> مِن شَرِّ الْوَسْوَاسِ الْخَنَّاسِ
> الَّذِي يُوَسْوِسُ فِي صُدُورِ النَّاسِ
> مِنَ الْجِنَّةِ وَالنَّاسِ
~ Surat al-Nas — "Say: I seek refuge in the Lord of mankind, the King of mankind, the God of mankind, from the evil of the retreating whisperer who whispers in the breasts of mankind, from among the jinn and mankind."
Al-Falaq seeks protection from harms that come from outside; al-Nas seeks protection from the whisper that comes from within. Together with al-Ikhlas, they surround the believer with faith and refuge.
