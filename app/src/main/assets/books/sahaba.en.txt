# Stars of the Companions

== Abu Bakr al-Siddiq: the truthful friend
Abu Bakr was a merchant of Makkah known for gentleness, generosity and an encyclopaedic knowledge of Arab lineage. When his friend Muhammad ﷺ told him that Allah had sent him as a prophet, he did not hesitate for a moment. The Prophet ﷺ later said: "I never invited anyone to Islam who did not pause and reflect, except Abu Bakr."
He used his wealth to buy tortured slaves and set them free — Bilal among them. When people mocked the story of the Night Journey, he said: "If he said it, he has spoken the truth; I believe him in something greater than that: news from heaven every morning and evening." From then on he was al-Siddiq.
He was the companion of the Hijrah, the one in the cave when the Prophet ﷺ said: "Do not grieve, Allah is with us." He gave all of his wealth for the expedition of Tabuk, and when asked what he had left for his family he replied: "Allah and His Messenger."
On the day the Prophet ﷺ died, while the strongest men were paralysed with grief, Abu Bakr stood and steadied the nation with a verse of the Qur'an. As the first caliph he held the community together through the wars of apostasy, ordered the Qur'an to be compiled and, after two years, died leaving no wealth — only an instruction that his salary be returned to the treasury.

~ The Prophet ﷺ · Bukhari and Muslim — "If I were to take an intimate friend, I would have taken Abu Bakr."
## Lesson
Faith that does not hesitate, and generosity that keeps nothing back.

== Umar ibn al-Khattab: the one who separated truth from falsehood
Umar was a fierce opponent of Islam. One day he set out with a sword to kill the Prophet ﷺ. On the way he was told that his own sister Fatimah had become Muslim. He burst into her house and struck her, then saw a page of Surat Ta-Ha and asked to read it. The words entered his heart, and he went straight to the Prophet ﷺ and declared his Islam. That day the Muslims prayed openly at the Ka'bah for the first time.
Umar was al-Faruq, the one who separates truth from falsehood. Several times his opinion was confirmed by revelation: on the veiling of the Prophet's wives, on taking the Station of Ibrahim as a place of prayer, and on the prisoners of Badr.
As the second caliph, he ruled the largest empire of his time from a mud house, patching his own garments and walking the streets of Madinah at night to see who was hungry. He carried flour on his own back to a widow's children and wept: "Will Umar be asked about them?" He established the Hijri calendar, the public treasury, the courts and the salaries of soldiers, and Jerusalem was opened to him without bloodshed by a treaty that protected its Christian inhabitants.
He was stabbed while leading the dawn prayer, and died asking his son to check whether he owed any debts. The Prophet ﷺ had said that if there were a prophet after him it would have been Umar.
## Lesson
Strength placed in the service of justice, and a heart that feared Allah more than it feared any man.

== Uthman ibn Affan: the possessor of two lights
Uthman was a wealthy, handsome and shy merchant who accepted Islam at the hands of Abu Bakr. He married the Prophet's daughter Ruqayyah, and after her death her sister Umm Kulthum — so he was called Dhu al-Nurayn, the possessor of two lights.
His generosity built the community. When the Muslims arrived in Madinah with no sweet water, he bought the well of Rumah and made it an endowment for everyone. When the mosque became too small, he bought the land to expand it. For the army of Tabuk he equipped hundreds of camels and gave a thousand gold coins; the Prophet ﷺ said: "Nothing Uthman does after today will harm him."
His modesty was such that the Prophet ﷺ said even the angels are shy of Uthman. He memorised the Qur'an and recited it through the night.
As the third caliph he sent copies of the standard Qur'an to every region, so that the whole nation would read one text. When rebels surrounded his house, he refused to let anyone shed blood in his defence, and he was killed while reading the Qur'an, its pages stained with his blood. He had chosen the honour of Madinah and the lives of Muslims over his own.
## Lesson
Wealth is a trust, and modesty is a crown.

== Ali ibn Abi Talib: the gate of the city of knowledge
Ali grew up in the Prophet's household, and was the first boy to accept Islam, at about ten years old. On the night of the Hijrah he slept in the Prophet's bed to deceive the assassins, and stayed behind to return the trusts people had left with the Prophet ﷺ.
He married Fatimah, the Prophet's youngest daughter, and their sons Hasan and Husayn were the Prophet's beloved grandsons. At Badr, Uhud and the Trench he fought in the front line. At Khaybar the Prophet ﷺ said: "Tomorrow I will give the banner to a man who loves Allah and His Messenger, and whom Allah and His Messenger love." That man was Ali.
His knowledge was legendary. Umar used to say: "I seek refuge in Allah from a problem that Ali is not present to solve." His speeches and sayings are treasures of wisdom: "People are asleep; when they die, they wake." "Do not be a slave to others when Allah has made you free."
As the fourth caliph he ruled through a time of civil strife with justice and pain, always preferring reconciliation over blood. He was killed at dawn on his way to the mosque of Kufa. He had lived as he taught: the world was in his hands, never in his heart.
## Lesson
Courage on the battlefield is one virtue; the courage to keep the world out of the heart is another.

== Bilal ibn Rabah: the voice of Islam
Bilal was an Abyssinian slave in Makkah whose master, Umayyah ibn Khalaf, tortured him for accepting Islam. He was laid on the burning sand at midday with a boulder on his chest and told to renounce his faith. He replied with a single word, repeated: "Ahad, Ahad" — One, One.
Abu Bakr bought him and set him free. In Madinah, when the adhan was instituted, the Prophet ﷺ chose Bilal for his voice, and five times a day the former slave called the city to prayer. He was so close to the Prophet ﷺ that he carried his water for ablution and slept near his door.
On the day Makkah was opened, the Prophet ﷺ ordered Bilal to climb the roof of the Ka'bah and give the call to prayer. The men who had once tortured him listened as his voice rang over their city.
The Prophet ﷺ once asked Bilal: "Tell me of the deed you hope most from, for I heard your footsteps ahead of me in Paradise." He said: "I have never made wudu without praying two units of prayer after it."
After the Prophet ﷺ died, Bilal could not bear to give the adhan in Madinah. He went to Syria and lived there until his death, and it is said that he called the adhan only once more, when he visited the Prophet's grave, and the whole city wept.
## Lesson
Islam raised a slave to a station that kings envy. Rank with Allah is by faith, not by colour or lineage.

== Salman al-Farisi: the seeker of truth
Salman was the son of a Persian nobleman, raised to tend the sacred fire of his people's religion. When he encountered Christian worship he felt drawn to it, left his father's house and travelled to Syria to serve a bishop. From one teacher to the next he was passed on, and the last one told him on his deathbed that the time of a prophet was near, in Arabia, in a land of date palms, with the seal of prophethood between his shoulders, who would accept gifts but not charity.
Salman set out with a caravan and was betrayed and sold into slavery in Yathrib — the land of date palms. When the Prophet ﷺ arrived, Salman tested him: he brought dates as charity and the Prophet ﷺ did not eat; he brought dates as a gift and he ate; and he saw the seal of prophethood on his back. Salman wept and embraced Islam. The Prophet ﷺ helped him pay for his freedom.
At the Battle of the Trench it was Salman who proposed digging a trench, a Persian tactic, and the Emigrants and Helpers each claimed him. The Prophet ﷺ said: "Salman is one of us, the people of the house."
Later he governed the city of Mada'in in Persia, living in humility, weaving baskets for his food and giving his salary away.
## Lesson
Whoever sincerely searches for the truth, Allah brings him to it, even across continents.

== Abu Hurayrah: the keeper of hadith
Abu Hurayrah came to Madinah in the seventh year of the Hijrah, a poor man from Yemen with nothing but love for the Prophet ﷺ. He attached himself to the Prophet's company day and night, staying among the People of the Bench, going hungry so that he would miss no word.
He complained once that he forgot what he heard. The Prophet ﷺ told him to spread his cloak, made a gesture into it and said: "Gather it." After that, he said, he never forgot anything. He became the most prolific narrator of hadith, with more than five thousand narrations preserved through him, memorised and checked by his students.
Abu Hurayrah was also gentle with his mother, who at first refused Islam. He asked the Prophet ﷺ to pray for her, and returned home to find her performing ablution to declare her faith.
He carried his knowledge with humility and humour, and he was frank about his motives: "People say Abu Hurayrah narrates too much. By Allah, if not for two verses in the Book of Allah, I would not narrate a single hadith" — the verses that condemn hiding knowledge.
## Lesson
Three years of devoted companionship produced a library that has taught every generation since. Company and sincerity multiply time.

== Khalid ibn al-Walid: the drawn sword of Allah
Khalid was the cavalry commander who turned the Battle of Uhud against the Muslims. Years later, after the Treaty of Hudaybiyyah, he reflected on the unstoppable rise of the message and travelled to Madinah to embrace Islam, together with Amr ibn al-As. The Prophet ﷺ welcomed him with the words: "Praise be to Allah who guided you; I always saw in you an intelligence that would lead to good."
At the Battle of Mu'tah, three commanders were killed one after another and the army was on the brink of destruction. Khalid took the banner, reorganised the ranks, broke nine swords in his hand, and brought the army home safely. The Prophet ﷺ named him that day Sayfullah, the Sword of Allah.
He led the campaigns against the apostates, the Persian frontier and then the Byzantines, winning at Yarmuk one of the decisive battles of history. He never lost a battle he commanded. When Umar removed him from command, he obeyed without complaint and continued to fight as an ordinary soldier, saying: "I fight for the Lord of Umar, not for Umar."
He died in his bed in Homs and wept: "There is no part of my body without the mark of a sword or an arrow, and here I am dying like a camel dies. May the eyes of cowards never sleep."
## Lesson
Talent becomes greatness when it submits to a cause and to discipline.

== Mus'ab ibn Umayr: the first ambassador
Mus'ab was the most elegant young man in Makkah, the darling of a wealthy mother who dressed him in the finest clothes and perfume. When he heard the Prophet ﷺ speak at the house of al-Arqam, he believed. His mother imprisoned him and cut him off completely; he chose faith over comfort and left with nothing.
The Prophet ﷺ sent him to Yathrib after the first pledge of Aqabah as his first ambassador, to teach the new Muslims the Qur'an. Within a year, gentle and wise, he had brought Islam into nearly every house of the city, including its chiefs Sa'd ibn Mu'adh and Usayd ibn Hudayr, who came to stop him and left as believers.
At Uhud he carried the banner and was martyred defending the Prophet ﷺ. When they came to shroud him, the only cloth he owned was too short: if they covered his head, his feet showed, and if they covered his feet, his head showed. The Prophet ﷺ told them to cover his head and place grass over his feet, and he stood over him and recited: "Among the believers are men who have been true to their covenant with Allah."
## Lesson
He gave up silk for the truth, and the truth gave him something silk never could.

== Abdullah ibn Mas'ud: the young shepherd who carried the Qur'an
Ibn Mas'ud was a thin young shepherd tending sheep outside Makkah when the Prophet ﷺ and Abu Bakr passed by. The Prophet ﷺ asked for milk, and the boy replied that the sheep were a trust and not his to give — an honesty that impressed the Prophet ﷺ. He became the sixth person to accept Islam.
He was the first to recite the Qur'an aloud at the Ka'bah before the pagans, and they beat him until his face bled; he said he would do it again. He learned seventy surahs directly from the mouth of the Prophet ﷺ, and the Prophet ﷺ said: "Whoever wishes to recite the Qur'an fresh as it was revealed, let him recite it in the manner of Ibn Umm Abd."
He was so close to the Prophet ﷺ that he carried his sandals, his siwak and his water, and people thought he was a member of the household. His thin legs were once mocked, and the Prophet ﷺ said: "By the One in whose hand is my soul, they are heavier in the scale than the mountain of Uhud."
Umar sent him to Kufa as a teacher, and from his circle grew one of the great schools of Islamic law. He used to say: "Knowledge is not narrating much; knowledge is fear of Allah."
## Lesson
Honesty in a small trust opened the door to the greatest trust: the Book of Allah.
