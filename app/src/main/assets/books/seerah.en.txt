# The Life of the Prophet ﷺ

== The world before his birth, and his childhood
In the sixth century, Makkah was a trading town built around the Ka'bah, the house Ibrahim had raised for the worship of one God. By now three hundred and sixty idols stood around it. Tribes fought over grazing land and honour, daughters were sometimes buried alive, and the weak had no protection except the strength of their clan.
In the year an army from Yemen marched on Makkah with an elephant to destroy the Ka'bah — and was destroyed by birds carrying stones, as Surat al-Fil recounts — Muhammad ﷺ was born, in the month of Rabi' al-Awwal. His father Abdullah had died before his birth. His grandfather Abdul-Muttalib, chief of the clan of Hashim, named him Muhammad, "the praised one".
As was the custom, he was nursed in the desert by Halimah of the tribe of Sa'd, and blessing entered her household with him. When he was six, his mother Aminah died on the road back from Yathrib. He was now an orphan of both parents, and the Qur'an would later remind him: "Did He not find you an orphan and give you shelter?"
His grandfather loved him deeply, but died when the boy was eight. His uncle Abu Talib took him in, and he grew up in a house that was honourable but poor, learning early what it means to have nothing but Allah.
! Allah chose an orphan to carry the final message, so that no one who has lost a parent should ever feel that greatness is closed to him.

== Youth: the trustworthy, and Khadijah
Muhammad ﷺ worked as a shepherd for the people of Makkah in his youth; later he said that every prophet had tended sheep. The work taught patience, watchfulness and care for the weak.
As a young man he earned a name that the whole city used: al-Amin, the Trustworthy, and al-Sadiq, the Truthful. He never lied, never worshipped an idol, never drank wine and never took part in the immoral entertainments of his tribe. He joined a pact of chivalry among the clans to defend the oppressed, and he later said he would have honoured it even in Islam.
When Quraysh rebuilt the Ka'bah and quarrelled over who should set the Black Stone in place, they agreed to let the next man to enter decide. It was Muhammad ﷺ. He spread his cloak, placed the stone on it, had a chief from each clan lift a corner, and set the stone with his own hands. A war was avoided by wisdom.
Khadijah bint Khuwaylid, a wealthy and noble widow, hired him to trade on her behalf in Syria. Impressed by his honesty and character, she proposed marriage. He was twenty-five; she was forty. Their marriage was a home of love and mutual support for twenty-five years, and all his children except one were born to her: al-Qasim and Abdullah, who died in infancy, and Zaynab, Ruqayyah, Umm Kulthum and Fatimah.
As he approached forty, he began to withdraw to the cave of Hira on a mountain outside Makkah, spending nights in reflection and worship of the One God.

== The first revelation
In Ramadan, in his fortieth year, while he was alone in the cave of Hira, the angel Jibril came to him and said: "Read!" He answered: "I am not able to read." The angel embraced him until he could hardly bear it, released him and repeated the command, three times, then recited:
> اقْرَأْ بِاسْمِ رَبِّكَ الَّذِي خَلَقَ ۝ خَلَقَ الْإِنسَانَ مِنْ عَلَقٍ ۝ اقْرَأْ وَرَبُّكَ الْأَكْرَمُ ۝ الَّذِي عَلَّمَ بِالْقَلَمِ ۝ عَلَّمَ الْإِنسَانَ مَا لَمْ يَعْلَمْ
~ Qur'an 96:1–5 — "Read in the name of your Lord who created; created man from a clinging clot. Read, and your Lord is the Most Generous, who taught by the pen, taught man what he did not know."
He came home to Khadijah trembling: "Cover me, cover me." When he told her what had happened and said he feared for himself, she gave the answer of a wise heart: "Never! By Allah, Allah will never disgrace you. You keep the ties of kinship, you carry the burdens of the weak, you give to the poor, you honour the guest and you help against the blows of fate." Then she took him to her cousin Waraqah, a Christian scholar, who told him this was the same angel who had come to Musa, and that his people would drive him out.
The first to believe were Khadijah, his cousin Ali (a boy of ten), his freed servant Zayd, and his closest friend Abu Bakr, who in turn brought Uthman, Talhah, al-Zubayr, Sa'd and Abdur-Rahman ibn Awf. For three years the call was made quietly, and a small community learned to pray in secret.

== Calling in Makkah and persecution
Then came the command: "Warn your nearest kin." The Prophet ﷺ climbed the hill of Safa and called the clans of Quraysh. "If I told you that horsemen were in the valley about to attack you, would you believe me?" "Yes," they said, "we have never known you to lie." "Then I am a warner to you before a severe punishment." His uncle Abu Lahab cursed him, and a surah of the Qur'an came down in reply.
Quraysh fought the message with ridicule, then with bribes — wealth, kingship, any woman he wished — and he answered: "If they placed the sun in my right hand and the moon in my left to abandon this matter, I would not abandon it until Allah makes it prevail or I perish." Then came torture. Bilal, an Abyssinian slave, was laid on burning sand with a rock on his chest, saying only: "One, One." Sumayyah, the mother of Ammar, was killed by Abu Jahl — the first martyr of Islam. Abu Bakr spent his fortune buying and freeing tortured slaves.
The Prophet ﷺ sent a group of believers to Abyssinia, whose Christian king, the Negus, refused to hand them over after hearing Ja'far recite the surah of Maryam. In Makkah, the clans of Hashim and al-Muttalib were boycotted for three years in a valley outside the city, until hunger drove them to eat leaves. Yet in those years Umar ibn al-Khattab and Hamzah, two of the strongest men in Makkah, embraced Islam, and the believers began to pray openly at the Ka'bah.

== The year of sorrow and the night journey
In the tenth year of prophethood, shortly after the boycott ended, Abu Talib died without accepting Islam, though he had protected his nephew to the end; and a few weeks later Khadijah died, the one who had believed in him when no one else did. The Prophet ﷺ called it the Year of Sorrow.
Seeking a new base, he walked to Ta'if and presented the message to its chiefs. They mocked him and set the children and slaves of the town to stone him until his sandals filled with blood. Resting under a vine outside the town, he made the prayer of a heart with nowhere to go but Allah: "O Allah, to You I complain of my weakness and my helplessness... If You are not angry with me, then I do not care."
Soon afterwards Allah honoured him with al-Isra and al-Mi'raj. In a single night he was carried from Makkah to the Masjid al-Aqsa in Jerusalem, where he led all the prophets in prayer, then taken up through the heavens, where he met Adam, Isa, Yahya, Yusuf, Idris, Harun, Musa and Ibrahim, saw Paradise and the Fire, and was brought to a place where he received the gift of the five daily prayers directly from Allah.
When he told Quraysh, they laughed; but Abu Bakr said: "If he said it, he has spoken the truth," and earned the title al-Siddiq. The night journey was a message: the doors of Makkah were closing, but the doors of the heavens were open.

== The Hijrah
Every pilgrimage season, the Prophet ﷺ presented Islam to the tribes who came to Makkah. In the eleventh year, six men from Yathrib listened and believed. The next year twelve came and pledged allegiance at Aqabah; the year after, seventy-three men and two women pledged to protect him as they protected their own families. Yathrib — soon to be called Madinah — was ready.
The Muslims began to leave Makkah quietly, family by family. Quraysh, alarmed, met in their council and decided that a young man from every clan would strike the Prophet ﷺ together, so that his blood would be spread among all the tribes. Jibril informed him. That night Ali slept in his bed under his cloak, and the Prophet ﷺ walked out through the ring of assassins reciting the Qur'an, and they did not see him.
He and Abu Bakr hid for three days in the cave of Thawr. The searchers came so close that Abu Bakr whispered: "If one of them looked at his feet, he would see us." The Prophet ﷺ answered: "What do you think of two whose third is Allah? Do not grieve; indeed Allah is with us."
On the road, the tracker Suraqah caught up with them for the reward, but his horse sank into the sand three times, and he turned back asking for a promise of safety — and was told he would one day wear the bracelets of the Persian emperor. He did.
The Prophet ﷺ arrived at Quba and built its mosque, then entered Madinah on a Friday, with the children of the city singing his welcome. The Muslim calendar begins from this migration, because it was the birth of a community.

== Building Madinah
The first thing the Prophet ﷺ built was a mosque — a simple structure of palm trunks and mud bricks that he helped to build with his own hands, with rooms beside it for his family. It was a place of prayer, a school, a court and a shelter for the poor Companions of the Bench.
Then he paired each Emigrant with a Helper from Madinah as brothers; the Helpers shared their homes, their orchards and their wealth so freely that the Qur'an praised them as people who "prefer others over themselves even when they are in need."
He wrote a charter that bound the Muslim tribes and the Jewish tribes of the city into one political community with mutual defence, freedom of religion and justice for all. He established the adhan, the call to prayer, in the voice of Bilal. In the second year the qiblah was changed from Jerusalem to the Ka'bah, fasting in Ramadan was prescribed, and zakat was organised.
Madinah became a model: a market without cheating, a society where a Bedouin could stop the head of state in the street and ask for his rights, and where the poor and the wealthy prayed shoulder to shoulder.
> وَاعْتَصِمُوا بِحَبْلِ اللَّهِ جَمِيعًا وَلَا تَفَرَّقُوا
~ Qur'an 3:103 — "And hold firmly to the rope of Allah all together, and do not become divided."

== Badr, Uhud and the Trench
Quraysh did not leave the Muslims in peace. In the second year after the Hijrah, the two sides met at the wells of Badr: three hundred and thirteen poorly equipped believers against nearly a thousand. The Prophet ﷺ prayed all night, his arms raised until his cloak slipped from his shoulders: "O Allah, if this group perishes, You will not be worshipped on earth." Allah sent angels, and the Muslims won a victory that changed Arabia. Abu Jahl and the chiefs who had tortured the believers fell that day.
A year later, Quraysh returned with three thousand men to Uhud. The Muslims were winning until the archers stationed on the hill left their posts to gather spoils, disobeying a clear order. Khalid ibn al-Walid, then still a pagan, swept around the hill. The Prophet ﷺ was wounded in the face, his tooth broken, and seventy Muslims were martyred, including his uncle Hamzah. The lesson of Uhud was written into the community forever: victory comes with obedience.
In the fifth year, an alliance of ten thousand men marched on Madinah. On the advice of Salman the Persian, the Muslims dug a trench across the open side of the city — the Prophet ﷺ digging with them, hungry, singing with them. The enemy camped for weeks, unable to cross. A cold wind and inner disputes broke the alliance, and they left without a battle. The Qur'an says: "Allah turned back the disbelievers in their rage; they gained no good."

== Hudaybiyyah and the conquest of Makkah
In the sixth year, the Prophet ﷺ set out with fourteen hundred companions to perform Umrah, unarmed. Quraysh blocked them at Hudaybiyyah. After tense negotiations, a treaty was signed with terms that looked humiliating: the Muslims would return this year and come back the next, and any Makkan who came to the Prophet ﷺ would be sent back. Umar was distressed; but Allah called it "a clear victory". Peace opened the roads, and in the two years that followed, more people entered Islam than in all the years before.
The Prophet ﷺ sent letters to the emperors of Byzantium and Persia, the Negus, the ruler of Egypt and the Arab princes, inviting them to Islam. In the seventh year the fortresses of Khaybar fell, and the Muslims performed the Umrah they had been promised.
When Quraysh broke the treaty by attacking the Prophet's allies, he marched on Makkah in the eighth year with ten thousand men. The city surrendered almost without a fight. He entered with his head bowed on his camel in humility, circled the Ka'bah, and had the idols broken down one by one, reciting: "Truth has come and falsehood has vanished." Then he faced the people who had tortured and exiled him for twenty years and asked: "What do you think I will do with you?" They said: "Good; you are a noble brother and the son of a noble brother." He said: "Go, for you are free."
Bilal climbed the Ka'bah and called the adhan over the city that had once burned him.

== The farewell pilgrimage and his passing
In the tenth year the Prophet ﷺ performed his only Hajj, with more than a hundred thousand Muslims. Standing at Arafat, he delivered the sermon that summarises his mission: the sanctity of life, property and honour; the abolition of usury and blood feuds of ignorance; the rights of women and the rights of husbands; the equality of Arab and non-Arab, black and white — "no one is superior except by taqwa"; and the command to hold to the Book of Allah. He asked: "Have I conveyed the message?" They said yes, and he raised his finger to the sky: "O Allah, bear witness." That day Allah revealed:
> الْيَوْمَ أَكْمَلْتُ لَكُمْ دِينَكُمْ وَأَتْمَمْتُ عَلَيْكُمْ نِعْمَتِي وَرَضِيتُ لَكُمُ الْإِسْلَامَ دِينًا
~ Qur'an 5:3 — "This day I have perfected for you your religion, completed My favour upon you, and approved for you Islam as religion."
Some months later, in the eleventh year, he fell ill with fever. When he could no longer lead the prayer, he ordered Abu Bakr to lead it. In his final days he warned against taking graves as places of worship, urged kindness to the weak, and repeated: "The prayer, the prayer, and those your right hands possess." On Monday, the twelfth of Rabi' al-Awwal, in the room of Aisha, with his head in her lap, he whispered: "Rather, the Highest Companion," and passed away.
Madinah was stunned. Umar could not accept it. Abu Bakr entered, kissed the Prophet's forehead, and then addressed the people: "Whoever worshipped Muhammad, Muhammad has died. And whoever worships Allah, Allah is Ever-Living and does not die." Then he recited: "Muhammad is not but a messenger; messengers have passed away before him."
He was buried where he died. He left no gold, no army in his name, no dynasty — only a Book, a Sunnah and a community that carried his light to the ends of the earth.
! May Allah's peace and blessings be upon him, his family and his companions.
