# Women of Light

== Khadijah: the first believer
Khadijah bint Khuwaylid was a woman of noble lineage and great wealth, a merchant who employed men to trade for her in Syria, respected in Makkah as al-Tahirah, the pure. When she hired Muhammad ﷺ, she found in him an honesty rarer than any profit, and she asked to marry him. He was twenty-five; she was forty and had been married before. Their home became the first home of Islam.
When he came down from the cave of Hira shaking, she was the one who wrapped him in a cloak and told him that Allah would never disgrace a man of his character. She was the first human being to believe in his prophethood — before any man — and she spent her wealth in support of the message until nothing remained.
Through the years of the boycott she went hungry with him in the valley, an elderly woman who had known every comfort. She died in the tenth year of prophethood, and he grieved for her so deeply that the year was named after his sorrow. Years later, when her sister's voice reminded him of hers, he trembled with emotion, and Aisha admitted she was jealous of no one as she was of Khadijah — a woman she had never met.
He said of her: "She believed in me when people disbelieved, she affirmed me when people called me a liar, she supported me with her wealth when people withheld, and Allah gave me children through her." Jibril himself sent her greetings of peace and the news of a house in Paradise of pearl, with no noise and no fatigue.
## Lesson
Faith is proved by support in the hardest days. Khadijah's greatness was not that she was rich, but that she gave it all.

== Aisha: the scholar of the nation
Aisha bint Abi Bakr grew up in the house of the Prophet ﷺ in Madinah and became, after his death, one of the greatest teachers of Islam. She had a sharp memory, a poet's command of Arabic and a jurist's mind. The senior companions came to her with their questions, and more than two thousand hadith are narrated through her, including the intimate details of the Prophet's worship and character that no one else could have known.
She narrated that he mended his own sandals, patched his clothes, milked the sheep and helped with the housework; that when he prayed at night his feet would swell; that he never struck a servant or a woman; and that his character "was the Qur'an".
When hypocrites slandered her, she waited in tears for a month until Allah revealed verses in Surat al-Nur declaring her innocence — verses recited until the end of time. She learned in that ordeal, she said, to trust Allah rather than to expect a revelation on her behalf.
She lived nearly fifty years after the Prophet ﷺ, teaching in the mosque of Madinah from behind a screen, correcting misunderstandings and preserving the Sunnah with precision. The Prophet ﷺ said: "The virtue of Aisha over other women is like the virtue of tharid over other dishes."
## Lesson
Knowledge has no gender. The nation learned half its religion from the daughter of Abu Bakr.

== Fatimah: the leader of the women of Paradise
Fatimah was the youngest daughter of the Prophet ﷺ and Khadijah, born in Makkah shortly before the revelation. As a girl she saw her father mocked and attacked; once, when pagans threw filth on him as he prostrated at the Ka'bah, she ran to clean him and wept, and he comforted her. She grew up carrying the weight of the message with him, and was called "the mother of her father" for her tenderness towards him.
She married Ali ibn Abi Talib in Madinah. Their home was poor: she ground grain until her hands blistered and carried water until her shoulders ached. When she asked her father for a servant, he taught her instead the remembrance said before sleep — thirty-three times Subhanallah, thirty-three Alhamdulillah, thirty-four Allahu Akbar — and told her it was better for her than a servant.
The Prophet ﷺ said: "Fatimah is a part of me; whoever angers her angers me." She resembled him in her walk and her speech. In his final illness he whispered to her that he would die, and she wept; then he whispered that she would be the first of his family to follow him and the leader of the women of Paradise, and she smiled.
She died six months after him, as he had told her, at about twenty-nine years old. Her sons Hasan and Husayn, the Prophet's grandsons, carried her line into history.
## Lesson
Poverty of the hand with richness of the heart. The best of women swept her own floor and remembered her Lord.

== Sumayyah: the first martyr
Sumayyah bint Khayyat was an elderly woman, a freed slave of humble means, the wife of Yasir and the mother of Ammar. The whole family embraced Islam early in Makkah and had no clan to protect them.
Abu Jahl and the men of Makhzum tortured the family in the desert heat, trying to force them to renounce their faith. The Prophet ﷺ would pass by, unable to stop the torment, and say: "Patience, family of Yasir; your appointment is Paradise."
Sumayyah refused to say a single word against her faith. In a fit of rage Abu Jahl struck her with a spear and killed her. She was the first person to die for Islam — a woman, elderly and poor, before any of the young warriors.
Yasir died under torture as well. Ammar survived to fight at Badr, to build the mosque of Madinah and to be described by the Prophet ﷺ as "filled with faith to the top of his head." Years later, at Badr, when Abu Jahl was killed, the Prophet ﷺ told Ammar: "Allah has killed the murderer of your mother."
## Lesson
The first blood shed for this religion was the blood of a woman who had nothing in this world and everything in the next.

== Asma bint Abi Bakr: she of the two belts
Asma was the elder daughter of Abu Bakr, sister of Aisha and wife of the companion al-Zubayr. She accepted Islam among the first, and her most famous moment came on the night of the Hijrah.
While Quraysh hunted the Prophet ﷺ and her father, it was Asma, then pregnant, who prepared their provisions and carried food to the cave of Thawr over the rocky hills. Having nothing to tie the bag with, she tore her belt in two and used one half, and the Prophet ﷺ named her Dhat al-Nitaqayn, She of the Two Belts. When Abu Jahl came demanding to know where her father was, she refused to speak, and he struck her face so hard her earring flew off.
In Madinah she lived a hard life with al-Zubayr, carrying date stones on her head from his land far outside the city, until her father sent her a servant. When she asked the Prophet ﷺ whether she could be kind to her pagan mother who came to visit, the answer came down in the Qur'an: kindness and justice to those who do not fight you for your religion.
She lived to a hundred, never losing a tooth or her sharp mind. At the end of her life, when her son Abdullah ibn al-Zubayr faced death at the hands of a tyrant and asked her counsel, she told him: "If you are upon truth, die upon it; and do not let the boys of Umayyah play with your head." He was killed, and she died days later.
## Lesson
Courage is not only for the battlefield. A pregnant woman climbing a mountain with food for two fugitives was carrying the future of Islam.

== Nusaybah bint Ka'b: the shield of Uhud
Nusaybah, known as Umm Umarah, was one of the two women who pledged allegiance to the Prophet ﷺ at the second pledge of Aqabah, in the dark, among seventy-three men. She belonged to the Helpers of Madinah and raised sons who fought beside her.
At the Battle of Uhud she came out to give water to the wounded. When the Muslim lines broke and the Prophet ﷺ was left exposed, she picked up a sword and a shield and stood in front of him, striking at anyone who approached. She received a deep wound in her shoulder from a horseman and kept fighting. The Prophet ﷺ said afterwards: "Wherever I turned, right or left, I saw her fighting in front of me."
He asked her what she wished for, and she asked that her family be his companions in Paradise. He prayed for it, and she said: "After this, I do not care what happens to me in this world."
She fought again at Hunayn, and in the wars against the false prophet Musaylimah she lost a hand and her son Habib was tortured to death for refusing to acknowledge the impostor. She lived on, honoured by Abu Bakr and Umar, a living reminder that the community was defended by its women as much as by its men.
## Lesson
When the Prophet ﷺ needed protection, a mother from the Helpers stood between him and the swords.
